Local review of the fixed-order lower bound

This is author self-review of NS83, not an independent audit. The result applies Burnol’s established dual-vector mechanism; no new-mechanism priority claim is made.

Normalization and domain: the unitary map W=I−J cancels the full exterior tail. The transformed atoms and target are supported in (0,1]. Their Mellin transforms have the stated powers of s. The phase uses its gamma-function expression at zeros, never a numerical quotient of two zero values. Normalized dilations change coefficients but not the finite span. Passing to complex coefficients leaves the optimum for the real target unchanged.

Boundary dual vectors: Burnol’s explicit expansion supplies the small-x singular term plus an O(x squared) remainder. The additional Hardy operators retain that remainder and add only logarithmic factors to the complete infinite tail. Truncation yields an L² norm limit as the spectral parameter approaches a critical zero. Thus exact orthogonality follows by continuity, without assuming that boundary evaluation itself is bounded on the whole space.

Multiplicity and target: the finite-zero Gram converges to separate Hilbert blocks; oscillatory cross-zero terms vanish after normalization. The inverse corner m squared is an analytic Cauchy-matrix identity, not an inference from eight numerical cases. The transformed target is O(x squared) at zero and has an integrable tail against the dual vectors, justifying the limit of the complete pairing. Its squared value at a critical zero gives the weight |ρ| to the power −2q.

Infinite zero sum: the projection argument is first applied to each finite zero set. Taking their supremum supplies the full lower bound. Classical unit-height zero counting bounds the multiplicity-weighted series absolutely. No RH assumption or interchange of an uncontrolled infinite Gram inverse is used.

Certified constants: FLINT’s zero constructor supplies certified enclosures for ten positive critical zeros. The additional check that zeta’s enclosure contains zero is a consistency check, not by itself an existence proof. Both conjugates have weight one, so larger unknown multiplicities and omitted zeros only increase the lower-bound constant. There is no finite-N onset, finite-N error certificate, or asymptotic equality.

Gain consequences: the product of successive error ratios gives a cumulative logarithmic budget. This excludes an eventual fixed fraction, and a/j with a greater than one, while allowing individual spikes. It does not conflict with the earlier uniform fraction of available block gain. Keeping q fixed is essential; changing q changes the target and norm.

Review corrections: the final text presents the forbidden decay rate as another consequence of the lower bound, not as a logical equivalence to the pointwise gain condition. The contents reserves a wider page-number column so long titles have clear separation. No unresolved mathematical issue was identified in this local review; independent review remains desirable.
