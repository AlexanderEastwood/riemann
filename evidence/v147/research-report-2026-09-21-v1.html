Level distribution is still an open input

NS-29 is completed as a scoped reduction: the exact zero expansion identifies a precise value-distribution statement, named Corrected Zero Level Distribution (ZLD). It is not proved by the zero theorems assessed. A separate packet must cover those levels with bounded original energy. Both uniform inputs remain open.

What had to change

A lower bound on the size of every sublevel set is insufficient. The needed statement controls the difference of sublevel measures for every interval of levels. With F(t) = measure{ξ in [0,X] : βa(ξ) < −t}, the sufficient input is

F(t₁) − F(t₂) ≥ κX(t₂ − t₁)/D,
0 ≤ t₁ < t₂ ≤ θD.

An explicit affine counterexample satisfies a cumulative lower bound yet omits half the levels. The manuscript also gives the exact density as a sum of inverse slopes at level crossings.

The packet needs Fourier density at least b/X on a set meeting at least an α fraction of each level band, and qa[g] ≤ Q. A positive fraction of the frequency interval alone, or an upper density bound, does not give this. Under the explicit hypotheses, any minorant with K distinct values satisfies

η(m) ≥ [bακθ²D/K − Q]+.

This separation is sufficient, not necessary for the original weighted condition: one suitably populated trough could suffice. The cofinal head-size rule N(a) must be prescribed. At a fixed window the negative set is bounded, so its proportion tends to zero if the head is enlarged arbitrarily. The global depth Da and the depth visible in [0,X] are kept distinct.

What the zero formula and v1.37 provide

The corrected complete zero sum equals βa exactly, with the strict-cutoff endpoint and trivial-zero terms retained. The explicit correction is bounded by

εa = ex + 4x−5/2 / [5(1 − x−2)].

A finite zero prefix still requires its Perron remainder. Even a small correction cannot simply be dropped when the requirement concerns arbitrarily narrow intervals of levels.

The primary-source assessment covers zero counting, zero-density estimates, Landau–Gonek sums, Montgomery pair correlation and its unconditional complex-zero extension. These stated results supply counts or averages, not the required signed distribution of the moving zero sum relative to its deepest value. No implication from RH, pair correlation, the density hypothesis or Lindelöf to ZLD is established here; neither is a converse. Logical independence is not claimed.

v1.37 proves Da → ∞ unconditionally. The new derivative estimate supplies local sublevel width with a window-dependent cost. The old RH probe yields the conditional lower bound

measure({βa < −t} ∩ supp wa)
≥ [1/(6π) − πt/a]+ / (Da − t).

At t = θDa this can be zero. It does not yield uniform band density or the mass of a physical packet.

The packet obstacle remains separate

A smooth pulse with broad Fourier coverage across [0,X] is constructed explicitly. Its prime-lag terms vanish exactly, and its original energy is log X + O(1). Thus this simple construction fails the uniform-Q requirement. This does not exclude other packets. Source projection retains both its pointwise Fourier cost and its energy cross term; odd parity has the same distribution argument without the even-source constraint.

The finding is a gap in the proposed obstruction proof. It is not failure of the physical form or of the valid weighted concentration inequality. Neither arithmetic band distribution (a) nor joint packet coverage and energy (b) is discharged, so packet spread alone cannot be declared the only remaining input. The route stays blocked, not closed.
