Independent derivation and attempted failure points

1. Signs and the full pole — exact identities. localization.tex:14–36,63–71,94–110. For the unitary transform, cf(t)=∫eiξt|f̂(ξ)|²dξ. The digamma integral, after substituting its integration variable by 2t, gives α(ξ)=ψ(1/4)−log π+2∫ρ(t)(1−cos ξt)dt. The prime form is −2∑wnRe cf(log n). The full convolution kernel 2 cosh((x−y)/2) gives +4∫₀∞cosh(t/2)Re cf(t)dt. Consequently replacing the correlation by Hcf gives the displayed defect: positive archimedean and prime coefficients and a negative full-pole coefficient. No 2π factor is missing, and the odd negative pole square has not been dropped.

2. Fixed-window positivity and translation — proved implication. localization.tex:38–61. Translation preserves the correlation and hence q. The product χsf has support inside (s−b,s+b); after recentering, this lies strictly inside (−log 4,log 4). The translated piece need not be even or source-orthogonal. Full positivity, obtained by combining the two reflection-invariant parity blocks, is precisely the input needed. A certificate only on an even source complement would not suffice, but that is not the input used here.

3. Integrability and interchange — proved implication. localization.tex:94–120. Normalization gives ∫‖χsf‖²ds=‖f‖² and ∫cχs f(t)ds=H(t)cf(t). Since χ is real and normalized, 1−H(t)=½‖χ(·+t)−χ‖²≥0 and is O(t²). Thus ρV is integrable at zero and at infinity. For compact smooth f, the prime correlations and pole correlations vanish outside a bounded t interval; only finitely many prime shifts remain. The original archimedean energy can be integrated first as a nonnegative squared difference. This avoids separating two divergent diagonal integrals. The fixed-window bound is valid and is expressly allowed to depend on the window.

4. Multiplier, closedness and high frequency — exact identity and proved implication. localization.tex:78–143. The multiplier equals α(ξ)+2∫ρV cos(ξt)dt−2∑wnH(log n)cos(ξ log n)+4∫cosh(t/2)H(t)cos(ξt)dt. The correction to α is uniformly bounded: its three parts are respectively an L¹ Fourier transform, a finite trigonometric sum and a compactly supported integrable transform. Thus mχ(ξ)=log(2+|ξ|)+Oχ(1). Its equality to q[eiξxχ] supplies nonnegativity using λ=4 positivity. Multiplication by √mχ is a closed operator, with the claimed logarithmic form domain. The asymptotic makes its even and odd restrictions unbounded; localized cosine and sine modulations test these separately. This constructs a closed positive localized form, not a closed positive realization of the global Weil form.

5. Dense near-radical input — adequate as cited. localization.tex:174–177, manuscript lem:v136-total-radicals at lines 12539–12579 and prop:v135-growing-radical beginning at line 12365. The needed facts are ordinary-norm convergence of smooth compact cutoffs and vanishing complete operator residual for each fixed vector in a dense linear translate span. The source has double-exponential tails, hence an entire nonzero Fourier transform with real zero set of measure zero; this supplies translation density. The residual proof retains all global prime shifts, both poles and the archimedean action. It uses a fixed finite translate representation, so it does not require uniform approximation coefficients. Reflection gives the two parity versions. No RH hypothesis or density in a global Weil form norm is introduced.

6. Weak graph limit — proved implication. localization.tex:169–200. Suppose the subcritical estimate held. One may take C≥0 without loss of generality. For T=√(1−θ)Tχ, it gives ‖Tra‖²≤q[ra]+C‖ra‖². The right side tends to C‖r‖². A subsequence of the images converges weakly; ra converges strongly. The graph of the closed linear operator is a closed linear subspace and is therefore weakly closed. Consequently r belongs to its domain and ‖Tr‖≤√C‖r‖. This holds for every element, hence every difference, in the dense linear span. Approximation then gives Cauchy images, and closedness forces an everywhere defined bounded operator. That contradicts the logarithmic multiplier. The argument does not attempt to extend the possibly unclosed global q by ordinary L² density.

7. Best constants and source restriction — proved implication. localization.tex:159–167,194–210. For a fixed window the defect is bounded, and subtracting θℒ cannot increase the supremum, so each best constant is finite. Nested supports give monotonicity. If their limit were finite, the excluded bound would hold on every compact test in the corresponding parity sector. Hence each tends to +∞. This is not a rate statement. The odd sector is automatically orthogonal to the actual even source. No monotonicity or density assertion is smuggled into the varying even source-complement spaces.

8. Critical coefficient and frequency cap — exact equivalences. localization.tex:212–240. At θ=1, ℒ−q≤ℒ+C‖f‖² is exactly q≥−C‖f‖². On the complete even source complement and odd sector this is exactly the CAE formulation in manuscript prop:v151-input-comparison, lines 11630–11643. The source residual and cor:v136-complement-floor, lines 12699–12723, are still necessary for the stated transfer. For a fixed nonnegative finite cap M, 0≤ℒχ,M≤M‖f‖²: a floor −C gives defect bound M+C, while a defect bound CM gives floor −CM. Capping changes the expression but provides no new estimate.
