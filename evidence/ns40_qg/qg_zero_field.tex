\subsection*{Quantization growth for the complete corrected zero field (NS-40)}
\label{ns40-sec-qg}

\paragraph{Status and object.}
The outcome is a precise unresolved input, together with proved sufficient
conditions and a proved cutoff obstruction. QG is not established for an
arithmetic cofinal family. No hypothesis below is asserted to be weaker
than RH, and no logical independence from zero statistics is claimed.
Keep the complete field $\mathcal Z_a=\beta_a$ of
\eqref{eq:v147-corrected-zero-field}, the global depth
$D_a=\|(\beta_a)_-\|_\infty$, and a specified cofinal rule
$X_a=\pi N(a)/a$. The negative pushforward $\widetilde m_a$ and
$\mathrm{QC}_K$ are exactly \eqref{eq:v149-measures} and
\eqref{eq:v149-qc}. The head range is an observation interval, not a
truncation of the symbol. All nontrivial zeros with multiplicity, the
strict endpoint, the trivial zeros, and the Perron limiting convention
remain part of the object. In particular the global depth is not a
head minimum or a zero-prefix minimum.

\begin{proposition}[Separated bands suffice for fixed-level growth]
\label{ns40-prop-bands}
Let $\nu$ be a finite positive measure on $[-D,0]$, $D>0$, and
use the same admissible level sets as in \eqref{eq:v149-qc}.
If $K+1$ Borel intervals $J_1,\ldots,J_{K+1}\subset[-D,0]$ have
pairwise distance at least $2h>0$ and $\nu(J_j)\ge m>0$ for every
$j$, then
\begin{equation}\label{ns40-eq-band-cost}
                 \mathrm{QC}_K(\nu)\ge hm.
\end{equation}
Consequently, fix a cofinal family and its rule $N(a)$. Suppose that
for every fixed $K$ there exist constants $d_K,m_K>0$ and, eventually,
$K+1$ intervals $J_{a,j}\subset[-D_a,0]$ with
\begin{equation}\label{ns40-eq-band-input}
 \operatorname{dist}(J_{a,i},J_{a,j})\ge2d_KD_a\quad(i\ne j),
 \qquad \widetilde m_a(J_{a,j})\ge m_K.
\end{equation}
Then $\mathrm{QC}_K(\widetilde m_a)\ge d_Km_KD_a\to\infty$ for
every fixed $K$, using the unconditional depth growth of
Corollary~\ref{cor:v137-scalar-no-go}. The constants may depend on $K$;
no estimate uniform in growing $K$ is asserted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For an admissible $\mathcal L$ the set where
$t-p_{\mathcal L}(t)<h$ is contained in
$\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal L}[\ell,\ell+h)$.
Each of these at most $K$ intervals meets at most one $J_j$, by
the separation hypothesis. At least one entire $J_j$ is missed.
The nonnegative loss is at least $h$ on that interval and its
integral is at least $hm$. Taking the infimum proves the assertion;
attainment of an optimizer is unnecessary.
\end{proof}

Condition~\eqref{ns40-eq-band-input} is an \emph{open sufficient input}
about values of the corrected field, not a theorem about its zeros.
It does not require a bounded density and is compatible with the
critical-value peaks in Proposition~\ref{prop:v149-critical-density}.
ZLD with any fixed $\theta>0$ implies it by choosing finitely many
separated subintervals of $[-\theta D_a,0]$ for each $K$.
The converse and necessity for QG are not asserted. More generally,
the first part suffices with varying $h_{a,K},m_{a,K}$ whenever
$h_{a,K}m_{a,K}\to\infty$; linear growth in $D_a$ is not required
by QG itself.

\begin{corollary}[A limiting-shape sufficient condition]
\label{ns40-cor-shape}
Let $\widehat m_a$ be the pushforward of $\widetilde m_a$ under
$t\mapsto t/D_a$, a measure of mass at most one on $[-1,0]$.
If, along the specified entire cofinal family, $\widehat m_a$
converges weakly to a finite positive measure with infinite support,
then \eqref{ns40-eq-band-input} and QG hold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each fixed $K$, choose $K+1$ distinct points of the limiting
support and small, relatively open interval neighborhoods whose
closures are positively separated. Each has positive limiting mass.
The lower bound for open sets under weak convergence gives a
common positive eventual lower mass bound for these finitely many
neighborhoods. Rescale them by $D_a$ and apply
Proposition~\ref{ns40-prop-bands}. This argument also permits atoms;
the support, not a density bound, is what matters.
\end{proof}
Neither existence nor infinite support of this limiting measure is
proved for the arithmetic family. Convergence on just one subsequence
would give only a conclusion on that subsequence.

\begin{proposition}[Cutoff dilution of the exact cost]
\label{ns40-prop-cutoff}
Fix $a$ with $D_a>0$ and put
$\ell_a=|\{\xi\ge0:\beta_a(\xi)<0\}|<\infty$.
Write $\mathrm{QC}_K^{X}$ for the exact cost observed on $[0,X]$,
always using the same global depth. Then
\begin{equation}\label{ns40-eq-cutoff-upper}
 0\le\mathrm{QC}_K^{X}\le\mathrm{QC}_1^{X}
 \le D_a\min(1,\ell_a/X).
\end{equation}
If $0<X\le Y$ and the entire positive-frequency negative set is
contained in $[0,X]$, then the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{ns40-eq-cutoff-scaling}
                  \mathrm{QC}_K^{Y}=\frac X Y\mathrm{QC}_K^{X}
\end{equation}
holds for every $K$. Given any prescribed positive function
$\delta_a\to0$, the integer rule
\begin{equation}\label{ns40-eq-diluting-rule}
 N(a)=\max\left(1,\left\lceil
             \frac{aD_a\ell_a}{\pi\delta_a}\right\rceil\right)
\end{equation}
gives $\mathrm{QC}_K^{X_a}\le\delta_a$ for all $K\ge1$.
One may increase this rule to meet any additional prescribed lower
bound on the head size without losing that upper bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each fixed window, $\beta_a$ tends to $+\infty$ at infinity,
so its negative set is bounded. The one-level loss is
$D_a+\beta_a(\xi)\in[0,D_a]$ there. This proves
\eqref{ns40-eq-cutoff-upper}. Once the negative set has been included,
the unnormalized integral is identical for $X$ and $Y$ for every
admissible $\mathcal L$; its infimum therefore scales exactly by
$X/Y$. Finally \eqref{ns40-eq-diluting-rule} gives
$X_a\ge D_a\ell_a/\delta_a$, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

This disproves QG asserted uniformly over all head rules, even for
the actual corrected field. It does not disprove QG for a particular
different rule. The displayed diluting rule is an analytic existence
construction, not an effective estimate of $\ell_a$ or $D_a$.
On any family satisfying QG it is necessary that
$D_a\ell_a/X_a\to\infty$, and more sharply
$D_a\widetilde m_a([-D_a,0])\to\infty$; neither is sufficient.
Changing $N(a)$ after forming a diagnostic changes the normalized
arithmetic demand. It does not change the complete Weil form.

\begin{lemma}[What a zero-prefix transfer would require]
\label{ns40-lem-prefix}
Let $F_a$ be a measurable real approximation on $[0,X_a]$, and
suppose $|F_a-\mathcal Z_a|\le r_a$ outside a measurable exceptional
set of length at most $\epsilon_aX_a$, with $r_a,\epsilon_a\ge0$.
For $-D_a\le u<v\le0$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{ns40-eq-prefix-band}
 \widetilde m_a((u,v))\ge
 \frac1{X_a}|\{\xi\in[0,X_a]:u+r_a<F_a(\xi)<v-r_a\}|
 -\epsilon_a.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Outside the exceptional set, membership in the inner band implies
$u<\mathcal Z_a<v$. Subtracting its possible exceptional mass proves
the inequality. An empty inner band gives no positive lower bound.
\end{proof}
For the finite symmetric zero sum plus the full correction $C_a$,
the missing error is exactly $-2\Re R_{x,T}(1/2-i\xi)$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:v146-beta-explicit}. To use this lemma one
must bound that real remainder on the prescribed growing head,
at a height admissible for that Perron formula (in particular $T>X_a$),
and prove the inner-band masses. Merely choosing $T>X_a$ is not a
remainder estimate. The small bound on $C_a$ in
\eqref{eq:v147-correction-size} bounds a different term.
For fixed separated normalized bands, $r_a=o(D_a)$ and an
exceptional fraction tending to zero would permit transfer of
positive inner-band masses; these error and mass inputs are absent.
The lemma does not replace the global depth by a prefix depth.

\paragraph{Primary-source assessment.}
Here each statement concerns the cited theorem in its stated range;
failure to derive QG is not a theorem that zero theory cannot imply it.

\emph{Counts.} Riemann--von Mangoldt gives
$N(T)=T\log(T/(2\pi e))/(2\pi)+O(\log T)$.
Hasanalizade--Shen--Wong give an explicit error
$0.1038\log T+0.2573\log\log T+9.3675$ for $T\ge e$
\cite[Corollary 1.2]{HasanalizadeShenWong2022}.
For widths of order $1/a$ at large height, the differenced main term
is of order $(\log T)/a$ but the available error is of order
$\log T$. This does not furnish local lower counts at that scale,
much less signed level-band masses of a sum with moving phases.

\emph{Density, including Ingham.} The classical bound is
$N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{3(1-\sigma)/(2-\sigma)}\log^5T$
\cite{Ingham1940}; the explicit version
\cite[Corollary 1]{ChourasiyaSimonic2025} has logarithmic exponent
$(7-5\sigma)/(2-\sigma)$, an additional logarithmic error, and
specified constants on its listed $\sigma$ intervals for
$T\ge3\cdot10^{12}$. These are upper counts. An off-line summand
here also carries $e^{2a(\Re\rho-1/2)}$. Its signed contribution
cannot be replaced by that of a critical-line zero. Even imposing
RH removes only this modulus issue, leaving value distribution and
global-depth normalization unproved. No QG implication from the
classical density hypothesis is established either.

\emph{Landau--Gonek.} The integer-$x>1$ uniform formula recalled in
\cite[Introduction, (1.1)]{Aryan2019} is
\[
 \sum_{0<\Im\rho<T}x^\rho=-\frac{T}{2\pi}\Lambda(x)
       +O\bigl(x\log(2xT)\log\log(3x)\bigr).
\]
This stated simplification must not be used for arbitrary real
$x=e^{2a}$. Even on integer-$x$ subfamilies it estimates a linear
sum, not the value distribution of its moving resolvent. The partial
summation identity in the NS-29 discussion shows the loss near the
moving ordinate if one takes absolute errors. Removable real
singularities are not poles of $\beta_a$. Uniform signed cancellation,
remainder control, and several negative band masses still need proof.

\emph{Montgomery.} The original theorem assumes RH and establishes
its normalized correlation asymptotic for fixed $0\le\alpha<1$,
uniformly on $[0,1-\epsilon]$ \cite[Section 1]{Montgomery1973}.
Use the corrected unconditional extension
\cite[Section 3, MT, (3.1)--(3.2)]{ns40-BGSTB2026}, not the old
multiplicative-error statement. With all zeros counted with
multiplicity, it gives, uniformly for $1\le x\le T$, $T\ge3$,
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal F(x,T)&:=\sum_{T<\Im\rho,\Im\rho'\le2T}
       x^{\rho-\rho'}\frac4{4-(\rho-\rho')^2},\\
 \mathcal F(x,T)&=\frac{T\log^2T}{2\pi x^2}
       \bigl(1+O((\log T)^{-1/2})\bigr)
       +\frac T{2\pi}\log x+O(T\sqrt{\log T}).
\end{align*}
It controls a quadratic correlation average with its particular
kernel. Taking $x=e^{2a}$ already restricts direct use to $T\ge e^{2a}$.
No comparison to the complete field's one-sided level cost on
$[0,X_a]$, with controlled errors in all growing parameters, is
provided by this formula. A variance estimate alone would not settle
the sign, separated-band masses, or scale relative to $D_a$.

\paragraph{The unresolved obstruction and its scope.}
The missing arithmetic statement is quantitative resistance of the
negative value distribution to approximation by any fixed number
of lower levels, on one declared cofinal family and head rule.
One sufficient route is \eqref{ns40-eq-band-input}; a zero-prefix
approach additionally needs \eqref{ns40-eq-prefix-band} with effective
errors and inner-band lower masses. None of the cited theorems supplies
this reduction here, and no established weaker-than-RH sufficient
hypothesis has been justified. This is a gap in the proposed argument,
not a counterexample for a chosen arithmetic family or a proof of
logical independence. The unconditional cutoff result only rejects
an assertion uniform over all head rules.

Even a future proof of QG would leave the separate source-admissible
bounded-energy all-Borel packet domination in
Assumption~\ref{ass:v149-qg}. Only with that input does
Proposition~\ref{prop:v149-weighted-cost} obstruct bounded level count.
No concentration no-go, negative Weil direction, positivity transfer,
new finite-window certificate, G2, or RH conclusion is obtained here.
