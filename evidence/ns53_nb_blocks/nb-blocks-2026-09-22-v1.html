An exact block formula, with the arithmetic gain still open

The approximation route needs convergence at any rate. This checkpoint identifies the exact improvement from adding integer dilations and the arithmetic estimate still missing.

RH and G2 remain open. The results below are exact identities and conditional implications; there is no new numerical certificate or convergence claim.

The correction to the earlier assessment

In the strong Nyman–Beurling criterion, the squared distance EN from χ = 1(0,1) to the span of ρn(x) = {1/(nx)}, n ≤ N, need only tend to zero. A bound such as EN = O((log N)−1/2) would suffice. The known lower bound does not require proving a matching sharp upper rate.

What adding a block actually buys

Let G describe the old vectors, D the new block, and C their cross terms. Let b and bB be their correlations with χ. The old optimal coefficients are c = G−1b. Two corrections are essential:

Residual correlations: h = bB − CTc.

Residual Gram matrix: S = D − CTG−1C.

Exact improvement: EN − EM = hTS−1h.

These formulas project the entire new block off the old span. They retain the cross terms and Gram conditioning. The derivation is standard Hilbert-space geometry specialized to the arithmetic dictionary, not a new estimate towards RH.

For κ = log(2π) − γ, an unconditional lower bound follows:

EN − EM ≥ ‖h‖² / [κ log(M/N)].

For a block N < n ≤ qN, the denominator is bounded independently of N. The unknown is the numerator: how much correlation the actual optimal residual retains with the new arithmetic block.

An elementary arithmetic checkpoint

The proof gives exact integral and convergent cell-series expressions for every Gram entry. It also expresses the whole residual through divisor sums on each interval j ≤ 1/x < j+1.

For the unsmoothed Möbius choice cn = −μ(n), the first N+1 cells alone contribute

(N+1) |∑n≤N μ(n)/n|².

This is a necessary penalty for that particular coefficient choice, not a lower bound on the optimal distance. The raw Möbius approximation is already known to fail in this norm; the checkpoint does not claim a new obstruction.

The open input

A sufficient residual block-correlation hypothesis is written explicitly in the proof. At Nj = qj, it asks for ‖hj‖² ≥ κ log q · αjENj, where 0 ≤ αj < 1 and ∑αj diverges. The hypothetical choice αj = 1/[2(j+1)] would already imply the slower rate above.

No such asymptotic estimate is proved. Nor is this sufficient hypothesis asserted to follow from RH. The trace bound may discard large gains in directions associated with small eigenvalues of S. An exact block quotient could succeed where the trace estimate fails.

The finding is a missing estimate. It establishes neither failure of the stated sufficient hypothesis nor failure of the approximation object. More finite distances would not settle it. No new Weil window or tail metric was introduced.
