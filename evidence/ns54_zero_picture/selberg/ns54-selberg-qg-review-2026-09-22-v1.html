A Gaussian law would help.
The arithmetic transfer is missing.

Selberg’s central limit theorem does not, as cited, prove the prescribed QG input for the complete Weil symbol. A precise Gaussian hypothesis for that symbol would imply fixed-level cost growth and rule out uniform population of bands at a fixed fraction of its global depth. Neither arithmetic conclusion has been established here.

Classification: exact identities and proved conditional measure-law implications, plus named open arithmetic inputs. This is an analytic review. No numerical window, complementary signed lower bound, G2 or RH result is claimed.

What the proposed argument must change

MAJOR — Match the observable. The exact symbol βa is not S(t), the argument fluctuation of zeta. Its finite prime formula differentiates a logarithmic sine polynomial and retains separate smooth terms. On RH its zero formula is a sinc statistic of zero density; the centered part involves a derivative kernel acting on S. Unconditionally it also depends on the real parts of zeros.

MAJOR — Match the limit and measure. QG samples uniformly on the prescribed interval [0, Xa], with Xa = πN(a)/a. A theorem at random heights in [T, 2T] does not specify this family. If that height band lies inside the head, its probability mass is multiplied by T/Xa. The simultaneous growth of a and T, the changing kernel, and the complete error must all be controlled.

MAJOR if the full obstruction is claimed — Supply the packet. Unweighted level statistics do not establish the separate source-admissible, bounded-energy packet with domination on every Borel set required by QG. That remains an independent missing estimate.

The conditional theorem that does survive

Let Ua be uniform on the actual prescribed head. Suppose βa(Ua)/ba converges in distribution to a Gaussian with finite mean and nonzero variance, where ba tends to infinity. Keep Da as the global negative depth.

Da/ba → ∞
QCK/ba → ∞ for every fixed K
ZLD fails for every fixed positive depth fraction.

The Gaussian’s negative tail forces the first limit. Every admissible quantizer must include −Da and round downward. With only finitely many levels, any bounded normalized cost would leave a fixed negative interval below every finite limiting level; positive probability remains there, while the available lower levels retreat to minus infinity. Its cost therefore diverges. Tightness also makes bands at a fixed fraction of Da carry vanishing probability, contradicting ZLD.

Rate correction: this is stronger than a lower bound proportional to ba, but gives no quantitative divergence rate. Saying the cost is asymptotic to the Gaussian spread is incorrect for this anchored one-sided definition. No moment convergence is needed for the lower-bound argument.

A centered CLT alone is insufficient: an uncontrolled large positive mean may leave no negative mass. A law conditional on negative values must restore the negative fraction; its band lower bound carries the factor baφ−(a).

What the available theorems cover

Radziwiłł–Soundararajan treats log|ζ|. Bourgade gives joint laws for finitely many shifted logarithms. These do not justify differentiating the limiting random field.

Maples–Rodgers does give an unconditional zero-statistic CLT. Its stated test class and diverging-variance condition exclude the unmodified sinc. Matching its spatial scale requires n(T) = log T/(2πL), L = 2a; the required n(T) → ∞ fails if log T is comparable to L. Bourgade–Kuan’s stated smooth-statistic theorem assumes RH and additional decay.

There is also an elementary check: at fixed a, the long-height variance of βa minus its archimedean term tends to 2Σn<e2aΛ(n)2/n, a finite constant. That limit does not have universal log log T variance. Its error constants are not uniform in a.

The exact remaining input

Prove a value law for the complete symbol on a specified cofinal head rule, with finite normalized centering and a diverging scale. A surrogate is sufficient if its normalized distribution error tends to zero and its difference from the complete symbol is o(ba) outside a set of relative measure o(1). Any discarded off-line zero contribution, Perron tail, and smoothing correction belongs in that error. The small endpoint/trivial correction is not a bound for the Perron remainder.
