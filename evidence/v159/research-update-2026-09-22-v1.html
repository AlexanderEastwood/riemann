A better fixed norm, with the arithmetic gap still explicit

One Mellin integration gives us a clean, unconditional block bound while preserving the RH criterion. The remaining task is to control the correlations of the actual optimized residual. RH and G2 remain open.

For the smoothed adjacent differences, the complete block trace is at most 3κ/(8N²), where κ = log(2π) − γ. The scaled trace tends to 3κ/8. This is an analytic result for all N, not an extrapolation from a table.

What changed

The previous raw norm ran into known large values of zeta. Explicit Euler corrections still meet a related obstruction when their grid product grows no faster than Nα, for fixed α < 1. That result applies to the specified correction family and its full coefficient norm; it does not exclude the optimal projection or the actual residual direction.

Smoothing gives a different route. Its adjacent differences are nonnegative averages, up to a unitary change of coordinates. Their norm bound is elementary. A bounded evaluation functional shows that an off-line zero would still prevent convergence, so we have preserved the mathematical target.

Our coefficient-mass remainder is over 2,000 times the leading norm on those blocks, so that triangle estimate gives no positive lower bound. The missing cancellation is stated explicitly.

Two shortcuts that do not finish the job

Smoothing the canonical Möbius interpolant does not repair its full-sequence convergence. A localized Laguerre test preserves the obstruction through any number of Mellin integrations. This does not exclude selected subsequences or optimized coefficients.

Increasing the smoothing order changes the measuring stick. Even one fixed function with one fixed coefficient then has relative squared error tending to zero unconditionally. At order 64 the certified relative squared error is about 1.38756%, while its absolute squared error exceeds 1035. The useful RH test keeps the smoothing order fixed and increases N.

Where I would work next

Keep one fixed integration and focus on an independent bound for the optimized residual’s signed remainder. The useful target is a non-summable sequence of guaranteed relative gains, even with a weak rate. Larger finite tables, a fixed sharp Möbius rule, and further normalization do not provide that estimate.
