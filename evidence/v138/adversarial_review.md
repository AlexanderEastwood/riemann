Reviewed September 21, 2026 against the complete v1.37 manuscript and `shifted_floor.py`. The user has expressly authorized lambda=8 for this diagnostic. This review does not establish a cofinal bound or close G2.

## Verdict and the useful change

The shifted complete-Schur construction is valid. A floor of -0.001 hardly changes the old scalar remote-cutoff cost. A **fixed moderate floor of -8**, however, makes the complete test inexpensive at the requested windows. This choice is justified by v1.36: a single finite uniform constant would suffice in a cofinal theorem. The numerical value need not be tiny.

The adopted construction holds delta=8, N=256, J=4096, r=16, tau=1/10, and Z=0 fixed across both parities and all three windows. Window-dependent coefficients, prime norm bounds, scalar tail weights, moments, and verification matrices must still be recomputed. This is a simpler use of an existing proof architecture, not a new independent arithmetic mechanism.

All six 160-bit complete runs saved `PASS`. The completed 256-bit replay in `replay_b256.json` reconstructs the **same** saved exact dyadic congruence and trial vectors and passes every complete proof gate. No fresh floating congruence proposal is generated by the replay.

## 1. Shifted Schur formula

Write W=[[F,B*],[B,T]] in an orthonormal head/tail split. For delta>=0,

    F_delta=F+delta I,  B_delta=B,  T_delta=T+delta I.

For any finite trial solve Z,

    K_delta=K_Z+delta(I+Z*Z),
    R_delta=R-delta Z.

If T>=diag(g_n) and g_(N+1)+delta>0, then

    T_delta^(-1)<=diag((g_n+delta)^(-1)).

The complete finite certificate is

    L_delta=K_delta
      -sum_(N<n<=J) R_delta,n* R_delta,n/(g_n+delta)
      -U_J/(g_(J+1)+delta) >=0.

It proves W>=-delta I on the full closed form domain. Rows beyond the finite support of Z do not change under the shift, and neither does their moment majorant U_J. Shifting K while forgetting the finite residual change would be wrong for nonzero Z.

For the adopted Z=0 construction, K_delta=F+delta I and R_delta=B exactly. The implemented unchanged residual is therefore correct. The remote-moment theorem uses M=N because G is the orthonormal head embedding; its proof only needs M<J. The old presentation's convenient intermediate strict inequality N<M is not essential when the solve is zero.

## 2. Scalar cutoff cost

With c=0 the fresh scalar weights are

    g_n=log(n/L)-E_L(2 pi n/L)-kappa_(lambda,N).

Their shifted positivity gate forces

    N+1 > L exp(M_phi,lambda-delta).

More generally, for the older W-cD comparison, the exponent is (M_phi,lambda-delta)/(1-c). A fixed shift does not remove the asymptotic exponential-in-lambda cost of this scalar comparison. It multiplies its leading scale by exp(-delta/(1-c)).

Independent 192-bit Arb evaluation used the exact rational active-set decisions in `prime_bound`, c=0, both parity corrections, and the strict signs of the chosen integer and its predecessor. It gave the following first positive remote cutoffs:

The N=1 entry is the boundary of the formula's admitted range N>=1. Every other listed predecessor had a strictly negative enclosure. These are tail gates only, not complete lower-floor certificates. In particular, delta=0.001 would leave the lambda=8 cost essentially unchanged from the old 124442/598623 scale.

The adopted fixed N=256 exceeds every delta=8 gate. This is the concrete computational benefit. It does not make N=256 a valid unbounded-window rule: the same formula eventually fails for every fixed N and delta.

## 4. Verification and margin reporting

The floating inverse-Cholesky factor is only a proposal. Converting its IEEE values to exact Arb dyadics, checking triangularity and nonzero diagonal, and verifying all outward Gershgorin rows of C L_delta C* proves positive definiteness of the underlying exact lower matrix. An interval matrix need not itself be exactly symmetric entry-by-entry; it encloses the exact Hermitian expression, and the row absolute-value bound remains valid.

The displayed whitening row margins near one must **not** be presented as invariant certificate margins or as evidence that headroom is stable across windows. The congruence was constructed from L_delta itself, so values near one are expected by design.

The parent's proposed separate generalized-margin lower bound is valid. If exact saved C satisfies

    C L_delta C* >= m I,
    C K_delta C* <= M I,

with certified m>0 and M>0, then

    L_delta >= (m/M) K_delta,
    1-lambda_max(U_delta,K_delta) >= m/M.

Here U_delta=K_delta-L_delta is the complete inverse-correction upper enclosure. Use a certified lower value for m and upper value for M. The resulting ratio is a lower bound on the coordinate-invariant margin; its tightness depends on the chosen bounding method. It is not the exact generalized eigenvalue.

## 5. What spectral brackets diagnose

A complete certified floor ell_lambda gives

    ell_lambda <= lambda_min(W_lambda).

An exact finite-support vector v gives a complete Rayleigh upper bound

    lambda_min(W_lambda) <= q_lambda[v]/||v||².

No omitted row enters that vector's quadratic pairing, so an interval upper bound on this quotient is valid for the full operator. Combine the two parity lower bounds using their minimum; combine available Rayleigh upper bounds using their minimum.

The correct interpretation is:

* A deteriorating lower certificate alone diagnoses a weaker enclosure, not increasingly negative actual eigenvalues.
* A positive Rayleigh upper bound near zero says there is small energy. It does not prove positivity or exclude a negative ground state.
* A failed lower certificate at -delta does not prove lambda_min<-delta. A separate trial with certified quotient below -delta would do that.
* A certified negative complete Weil Rayleigh quotient would be a genuine negative compact-window direction, a far stronger outcome than a trend in lower-bound artifacts. It would require extraordinary independent validation because Weil positivity is equivalent to RH.
* The bracket [-8, small positive number] is coarse but honest. It tests whether the same complete architecture transfers with a fixed floor. Three such brackets do not establish the cofinal premise of v1.36.

## 6. What was and was not changed

The improvement in practical cost comes from using a **fixed non-small additive floor** permitted by v1.36. It does not repair the old lambda=5 inequality W>=10^(-8)D on Q_16, which remains disproved. That positive weighted metric and W>=-8I are different statements.

The frozen head/remote sizes, moment order, Young parameter and zero solve are unchanged across the three windows. The finite congruence witnesses naturally differ because the actual operators differ; generating and verifying each such witness is part of the fixed procedure, not a new analytic tuning rule.

No genuinely new signed arithmetic mechanism was found in this review. The existing complete Schur/moment argument becomes affordable for these finite floors. Its remaining asymptotic limitation is explicit: with delta=8 fixed, the old scalar remote comparison still requires exponentially growing cutoffs. A successful finite list supplies neither a uniform error estimate nor an unbounded-window certificate.
