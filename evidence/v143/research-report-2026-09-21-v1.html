The limiting input, and the required lower bound

The direct limit is Cℓρ, an upper ground-energy budget. It is not the old ordinary projection error √(ρ/b) ≈ 1.5664 × 10−4, where b = 10−67 is the even-sector separator. A lower bound on μ0 does not directly improve √(Cℓρ)/d.

A certified lower bound 0 ≤ ℓ0 ≤ μ0 has a different role. Writing Δ = ρ − ℓ0 and e = f − h, spectral orthogonality gives q[e] ≤ bΔ/(b − ℓ0). The evaluator estimate then controls the trial-to-ground value error. Allocating a zero uncertainty ε = 10−71 entirely to that error requires

ρ − ℓ0 ≤ (dε)²(b − ρ) / (Cℓb − (dε)²)
= 3.20320065696758… × 10−153.

A gap at most 3.2031 × 10−153 is an outward sufficient condition for that transfer term. This means a relative Rayleigh gap about 1.3055 × 10−78; merely proving μ0 positive, or bounding it below by 10−153, would not suffice. The archive lacks such a lower-energy estimate. With only the old ordinary-norm projection estimate, the gap requirement would be about 9.233248 × 10−216.

That missing estimate would still not suffice for a γ1-centered enclosure. The frozen trial has the certified value 1.0641 × 10−40 < f̂(γ1) < 1.0642 × 10−40. A tiny transfer uncertainty around this trial does not remove its center value. A suitable trial center or another controlled cancellation would also be needed. The hypothetical lower bound is not claimed or pursued here.

The obstruction is in the bound

The separately certified finite N = 120 benchmark has discrepancy in (2.9250, 2.9251) × 10−71. It was prepared during the original Task B and is retained as finite-compression evidence only. It is not the midpoint diagnostic and is not a complete-ground value.

That benchmark scale is more than 1038 below the resolution of the displayed complete comparison. Even formally omitting its nonnegative tail term leaves a radius about 2.5174 × 10−35. Increasing arithmetic precision does not remove these analytic budgets.

This is a failure of the stated bounds to resolve the benchmark scale, not a failure of the object. “Resolution floor” is scoped to these archived estimates with their inputs unchanged. No theorem rules out every different argument using the same data, and no claim assigns the actual complete discrepancy the finite benchmark’s magnitude.

The propositions explicitly state their complete-operator, Schur, spectral, normalization and derivative hypotheses. They prove a finite-window instance of zero proximity. They do not establish CCM’s source-to-ground approximation, an unbounded sequence of windows, or convergence of normalized transforms to Riemann’s Ξ.
