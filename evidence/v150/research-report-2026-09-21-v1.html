An explicit positive weight, with a proved limit on energy loss

What was established

The comparison weight h is the existing Gaussian arithmetic radical, written explicitly as a prime-independent Gaussian sum. It is even, smooth and strictly positive, with proved decay bounds and exact pole mass ∫cosh(x/2)h(x)dx = 1/√3. It is not substituted for the actual repaired source.

The last, negative square remains in the odd sector. The finite-window identity retains every exterior edge. The proof establishes convergence on compact tests and extension to each fixed-window form domain, without assuming a globally positive operator.

What had to change

A generic strict absorption estimate would spend a fraction δ of the positive transformed energy. For this weight, an inward prime-2 edge on translated compact tests already costs at least κ exp(γλ²), with explicit positive κ and γ. On the same unit tests the original form is at most Ba = A + (8a+1)ea + 2a, where a = log λ and A is finite and independent of the window. Consequently every error bound of the stated form obeys

ηa ≥ [δaκ exp(γλ²) − Ba]+.

A uniformly bounded error therefore requires δa = O(λ log λ · exp(−γλ²)). Deleting the prime-2 transformed energy alone has the same obstruction. Two even test directions impose the actual rank-one source constraint exactly; odd tests are automatically source-orthogonal. Every linear combination and its cross terms are covered.

This is failure of the proposed lower comparison, not failure of the physical Weil form. Only the fixed-fraction loss and specified prime-2 deletion shortcuts for this weight are closed. The full coefficient-one comparison and other choices of weight remain available but unproved.

The missing input

It is exactly the existing uniform complement-floor target in these coordinates. Its identity does not provide an independent proof.
