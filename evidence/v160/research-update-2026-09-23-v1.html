Why the correction matters

The elementary part of the Gram formula assigns the wrong leading energy to two very close dilations. Subtracting an explicit term, d/max(u,v), fixes that mismatch. In adjacent-difference coordinates, the correction is exactly diagonal.

The corrected kernel still cannot bound the actual kernel uniformly over every coefficient vector: known zeta zeros and large values prevent that. Our experiment uses it only to choose coefficients, then retains the true arithmetic energy. This is why the finite improvement is compatible with the comparison obstruction.

Other routes tested

  • Canonical cancellation: exact signed cells and complete tail bounds retain the open growth criterion. After an explicit centering, cancellation between the two complete Abel vectors cannot improve their power exponent. Useful cancellation must occur inside the arithmetic integral or cell sums.
  • Positive renewal: the geometric averaging operator has a controlled inverse, but it transfers the same growth exponent. The signed forcing still needs an RH-equivalent estimate. Its critical weighted total energy diverges; this does not say the canonical norm itself diverges.
  • Endpoint approximation: the actual signed remainder exceeds the leading model at sizes 64 and 128. At size 128, both endpoint directions together capture only about 3.3% of the available gain. Treating that remainder as a small correction is unsupported on those blocks.

The next mathematical target

The selected-direction formula separates two quantities: the residual’s model-inverted numerator relative to its error, and the true energy cost of the chosen direction. At size 256 they are approximately 0.76685 and 9.48848, giving the observed 0.080819 relative improvement.

We need independent bounds on those quantities along arbitrarily large blocks, strong enough that the relative improvements have a divergent sum. That would force convergence in one fixed norm and hence RH. Neither the finite table nor the elementary kernel supplies these bounds.
