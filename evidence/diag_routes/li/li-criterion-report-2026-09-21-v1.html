Li's criterion against the certified W_4 ≥ 0 window

Research-plus-computation note for the manuscript "Fixed-Space Prime Compatibility in Burnol's Sonine Quotients" (v1.38). Written 2026-09-21. Work directory /tmp/licriterion/; nothing in ~/riemann was touched.

Labels: exact identity or definition · proved theorem (hypotheses stated) · numerical · heuristic · open.

(a) Bottom line

Nothing transfers. The Bombieri–Lagarias test function whose Weil form is the Li coefficient λ_n is g_n(x) = L^{(1)}_{n-1}(−log x) on 0 < x < 1 and 0 for x > 1: its multiplicative support is the half-line (0,1] (log-coordinate (−∞,0]), its Mellin transform G_n(s) = 1 − (1 − 1/s)^n is a rational function with a pole of order n at s = 0, and the Li class (rational functions vanishing at ∞ with poles only at 0, 1) meets the Paley–Wiener class of every finite window only in {0} — so no certificate W_λ ≥ 0, at λ = 4 or on any finite list of windows, certifies any λ_n, and the pole that is removed by windowing is exactly the mechanism (|1−1/ρ|^n growth) by which λ_n detects an off-line zero. What W_4 ≥ 0 does give is the sign of a windowed coefficient λ_n^{[log 16]} (the same Laguerre polynomial cut off at t = 2 log 4, which lies in the manuscript's closed semilocal domain), an object that numerically tracks λ_n to about 1% for n ≤ 60 but with a sign-indefinite difference, and whose positivity carries no information about λ_n itself. Li's criterion offers no purchase on the uniformity wall: each single λ_n ≥ 0 is implied by finite zero verification (elementary bound below), and the whole difficulty sits in n → ∞, where it becomes control of an n-fold binomial transform of the Laurent coefficients of ζ'/ζ at s = 1 — the same wall in a different coordinate.

(b) Statements, support analysis, and the windowed statement

Li's criterion proved

For n ∈ ℤ put (zeros of ξ with multiplicity, sum taken as lim_{T→∞} Σ_{|ρ|≤T})

λ_n = Σ_ρ [ 1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n ]  =  (1/(n−1)!) d^n/ds^n [ s^{n−1} log ξ(s) ] |_{s=1}   (n ≥ 1),
i.e.  d/dz log ξ(1/(1−z)) = Σ_{n≥1} λ_n z^{n−1}.

Theorem (Li 1997). RH ⇔ λ_n ≥ 0 for all n ≥ 1. [X.-J. Li, "The positivity of a sequence of numbers and the Riemann hypothesis", J. Number Theory 65 (1997) 325–333.] The same numbers appear, as λ_{−n}/n, in Keiper [J. B. Keiper, "Power series expansions of Riemann's ξ function", Math. Comp. 58 (1992) 765–773], who noted that RH implies their nonnegativity.

Bombieri–Lagarias proved

[E. Bombieri, J. C. Lagarias, "Complements to Li's criterion for the Riemann hypothesis", J. Number Theory 77 (1999) 274–287.] Three results:

  1. Generalized Li criterion (Thm 1). For any multiset Z ⊂ ℂ∖{0,1} with Σ_{ρ∈Z} Re ρ /(1+|ρ|)^2 < ∞, invariant under ρ ↦ 1 − ρ̄: all Re ρ = 1/2 ⇔ Re λ_n(Z) ≥ 0 for all n ≥ 1. So Li positivity is a statement about multisets, not about ζ.
  2. Arithmetic formula (Thm 2) exact, via the explicit formula, λ_n = 1 + S_∞(n) − S_f(n) (pole, archimedean, finite places). Written out (Coffey's normalization; verified below to 63 digits against the definition): 
    λ_n = 1 − (n/2)(γ + log π + 2 log 2) + Σ_{j=2}^{n} (−1)^j C(n,j) (1 − 2^{−j}) ζ(j) − Σ_{j=1}^{n} C(n,j) η_{j−1},
ζ'(s)/ζ(s) = −1/(s−1) − Σ_{k≥0} η_k (s−1)^k,    η_0 = −γ.
  3. Weil-positivity reading (their §3, Lemma 2; restated in Lagarias 2007 Thm 3.1 and App. A). With f̂(s) = ∫_0^∞ f(x) x^s dx/x and Weil scalar product ⟨F,G⟩_W = Σ_ρ F(ρ) \overline{G(1−ρ̄)}, the Li test functions are 
    G_n(s) = 1 − (1 − 1/s)^n = ĝ_n(s),
g_n(x) = P_n(log x)  (0 < x < 1),   g_n(1) = n/2,   g_n(x) = 0  (x > 1),
P_n(u) = Σ_{j=1}^{n} C(n,j) u^{j−1}/(j−1)!  =  L^{(1)}_{n−1}(−u)   (associated Laguerre polynomial),
    and ⟨G_n, G_m⟩_W = λ_n + λ_{−m} − λ_{n−m}, in particular ‖G_n‖²_W = λ_n + λ_{−n} = 2 λ_n. In log coordinate t = −log x ≥ 0 this is the Laplace identity ∫_0^∞ L^{(1)}_{n−1}(t) e^{−st} dt = 1 − (1−1/s)^n (from s^{−k} = ∫ t^{k−1}e^{−st}dt/(k−1)! and Σ_k C(n,k)(−1)^{k−1}t^{k−1}/(k−1)! = −L_n'(t) = L^{(1)}_{n−1}(t)). Hence: Li's criterion is Weil positivity restricted to the Li class 𝓛 = {rational F, F(∞)=0, poles ⊂ {0,1}}, for which {G_n}_{n≠0} is a basis. [J. C. Lagarias, "Li coefficients for automorphic L-functions", Ann. Inst. Fourier 57 (2007) 1689–1740, arXiv:math/0404394, §3 and Appendix A.]

The sharp question: support proved

  • Multiplicative support of g_n is (0,1], i.e. (−∞,0] in the log coordinate. Not compact for any n; the non-compact end is x → 0 (t → +∞ in the Laplace variable), where L^{(1)}_{n−1}(t) grows like t^{n−1}. In the manuscript's normalization (test f supported in [λ^{−1}, λ]) one would need λ^{−1} = 0.
  • Transform side. G_n is rational with a pole of order n at s = 0. The Mellin transform of any test function supported in [λ^{−1},λ] is entire of exponential type log λ (Paley–Wiener). A rational function that is entire is a polynomial, and 𝓛 contains no nonzero polynomial (its elements vanish at ∞). Therefore 𝓛 ∩ PW_a = {0} for every a: no Li coefficient is the Weil form of any windowed test function, for any window.
  • Not even on the zeros. One could hope for a windowed F with F(ρ) = G_n(ρ) on the zero multiset (same Weil value as an identity in the zeros). Impossible: F − G_n would then vanish on all zeros; multiply by s^n: s^n F(s) − s^n G_n(s) is entire of exponential type a, of polynomial growth on vertical lines, vanishing on all zeros, whose counting function N(T) ~ (T/2π) log T exceeds the Cartwright-class bound O(a T) for the zeros of a nonzero entire function of exponential type a with such growth. (Standard Cartwright/Levinson density theorem; details routine.)
  • Why this is the mechanism, not a technicality. For an off-line zero ρ = β + iγ, the quadruple {ρ, ρ̄, 1−ρ, 1−ρ̄} contributes 4 − 4 cosh(n u) cos(n θ) to λ_n, with u = log|1−1/ρ|, |u| ≈ |1−2β|/(2γ²). The exponential e^{n|u|} — the only way λ_n can ever go negative — comes from |1−1/ρ| ≠ 1, i.e. from the pole of G_n at 0. Any windowed replacement Φ of G_n is entire, so |Φ(ρ)Φ(1−ρ)| is bounded polynomially in n for each fixed ρ; windowing removes exactly the detector.

The windowed statement proved (modulo the certificate and the domain check)

Let a = log 4 and b = 2a = log 16. Define, for n ≥ 1,

f_n^{[b]}(t) = L^{(1)}_{n−1}(t)·1_{[0,b]}(t),      Φ_n^{[b]}(s) = ∫_0^b L^{(1)}_{n−1}(t) e^{−st} dt
                = Σ_{k=1}^{n} C(n,k)(−1)^{k−1} s^{−k} [ 1 − e^{−sb} e_{k−1}(sb) ],   e_m(z) = Σ_{j≤m} z^j/j!,
λ_n^{[b]} := ½ Σ_ρ Φ_n^{[b]}(ρ) Φ_n^{[b]}(1−ρ)   ( = Σ_{conjugate pairs} |Φ_n^{[b]}(ρ)|² on RH;  Φ_n^{[∞]} = G_n, λ_n^{[∞]} = λ_n ).

Translate f_n^{[b]} by a so that its support is [−a, a] (multiplicatively [1/4, 4]); a translation multiplies the Mellin transform by e^{−as} and hence the Weil value by the positive constant e^{−a}, so the sign is unchanged. The translated function is compactly supported, piecewise polynomial with two jumps (values n at t=0 and L^{(1)}_{n−1}(b) at t=b), hence BV, hence in the manuscript's closed semilocal form domain (v1.38 §"The semilocal domain and truncation": compactly supported BV ⇒ ∫(1+log(2+|ξ|))|f̂|² < ∞). Therefore:

Corollary of W_4 ≥ 0 (both parities). λ_n^{[log 16]} ≥ 0 for every n ≥ 1. Equivalently, by the explicit formula, an explicit inequality among the archimedean term, the pole term, and the prime powers 2,3,4,5,7,8,9,11,13 only. (If the certified class is instead only the even or odd sector separately, split f_n^{[b]} into its even and odd parts about the window center; both parts are certified, and the cross term vanishes by parity.)

What it does not say. λ_n − λ_n^{[b]} is a zero sum with no sign control and, as the numbers below show, oscillates in sign as b grows. So W_4 ≥ 0 gives no lower bound on any λ_n, and the same holds for any finite list of windows. Passing from λ_n^{[b]} to λ_n along b → ∞ requires W_λ ≥ −ε_λ with ε_λ → 0 — i.e. exactly prop:v121-cofinal-rh. Li's criterion adds nothing to that.

(c) λ_n for n = 1..60, three ways

Route A (definition). Taylor coefficients of d/dz log ξ(1/(1−z)) = (1−z)^{−2}·(ξ'/ξ)(1/(1−z)) with ξ'/ξ(s) = 1/s + 1/(s−1) − ½log π + ½ψ(s/2) + ζ'(s)/ζ(s), by a Cauchy integral (trapezoid rule on a circle; no branch of log needed). mpmath, 80 digits. Stability: radius 0.5 / 256 nodes vs radius 0.7 / 320 nodes agree to 1.9e−47; spurious imaginary parts ≤ 7e−65. The circle |z| ≤ 0.7 maps to |s − 1.96| ≤ 1.37, which contains no zero (first zero at height 14.13), so the coefficients are the true Taylor coefficients unconditionally.

Route B (Bombieri–Lagarias arithmetic formula) with η_k from a Cauchy integral of ζ'/ζ(s) + 1/(s−1) on |s−1| = 1 (η_0 = −γ reproduced to 20 digits). Max |A − B| over n ≤ 60: 1.1e−63 — the formula and its normalization are verified.

Route C (zeros). λ_n = Σ_{k≤2000} 2 Re[1 − (1 − 1/ρ_k)^n] with ρ_k = ½ + iγ_k from mpmath.zetazero(k) (30 digits; γ_2000 = 2515.28…), plus a smooth tail ∫_{γ_2000}^∞ [2 − 2cos(nθ(t))] (1/2π) log(t/2π) dt, θ(t) = arg(1 − 1/(½+it)) = 2 arctan(1/2t). The tail is 1.9% (n=1) to 2.8% (n=60) of the value; without it the zero sum is visibly low. With it, routes A and C agree to 5 significant digits for every n ≤ 60 (worst relative discrepancy 6.2e−6, at n=8; absolute 3.5e−4 at n=60), the residual being the discreteness of zeros above 2515 that the smooth tail averages out. Stability check: repeating with the first 4000 zeros (γ_4000 = 4638.24…, tail 1.2%–1.7%) reduces the worst relative discrepancy to 1.5e−6 (absolute 8.3e−5 at n=60), a 4× reduction on doubling the zero count, as expected for a tail-discretization residual. (d) Does Li's criterion offer purchase on the uniformity wall?

No. Three honest points.

  1. Each single λ_n ≥ 0 is a finite-height statement proved (elementary sketch). On-line pairs contribute 4 sin²(n·arctan(1/2γ)) ≥ 0; an off-line quadruple at height γ contributes ≥ −8 sinh²(n/(4γ²)). If all zeros with |γ| ≤ T lie on the line (T ≥ 100) and n ≤ T/8, then |u| ≤ 1/(2γ²) gives a total hypothetical off-line loss ≤ 0.06 n² log T/T³ (using N(t) ≤ (t/2π) log t), while each on-line pair with γ ∈ [T/2, T] contributes 4 sin²(n·arctan(1/2γ)) ≥ 0.98 n²/T² and there are more than T/10 such pairs, so the gain is ≥ 0.098 n²/T; the ratio gain/loss exceeds 1.6 T²/log T, hence λ_n > 0. (Grouping the ∗-convergent sum into conjugate/reflection quadruples is harmless: the boundary discrepancy is O(n/T) → 0.) With Platt–Trudgian's T = 3·10^{12} [D. Platt, T. Trudgian, "The Riemann hypothesis is true up to 3·10^{12}", Bull. LMS 53 (2021) 792–797, arXiv:2004.09765] this gives λ_n > 0 for n ≤ 3.7·10^{11}; heuristically the true sensitivity scale is n ~ γ²/(1−2β) (Lagarias 2007 §1 remarks that RH to height 10^9 should govern the first ~10^{16} coefficients) heuristic. So "λ_n ≥ 0 for n ≤ N" is the Li-side analogue of a window certificate, with the exchange rate index n ↔ zero height between n and n², versus window a ↔ height T* ≈ 2π e^{2a} (Zhu's law as used in COMPARISON.md; a = log 4 gives T* ≈ 100).
  2. The countable structure does not help; the difficulty moves entirely into n → ∞. λ_n ≥ 0 ∀n ⇔ S_f(n) ≤ 1 + S_∞(n) = (n/2) log n + cn + O(1) for all n, where S_f(n) = Σ_{j=1}^{n} C(n,j) η_{j−1} is an alternating binomial transform of the Laurent coefficients of ζ'/ζ at s = 1; individual terms are of size ~2^n while the sum is O(√n log n) on RH and exponentially large otherwise. Bounding that cancellation uniformly in n is RH, verbatim (Coffey 2005 frames RH as conjectured properties of the η_j; Lagarias 2007 Thm 6.1 shows S_f(n) is governed by zeros up to height √n). There is no known argument, and no structural reason, for the positivity of the n-th member to say anything about the (n+1)-st: the Li class is a basis of rational functions with poles at 0 and 1, and Weil positivity on it is positivity of the infinite Gram matrix (λ_n + λ_{−m} − λ_{n−m}), an object with no finite-rank approximation property that the window route lacks.
  3. Relation between the two families. The windowed forms and the Li forms are transverse subspaces of the Weil-form domain (entire of exponential type vs rational with poles at 0, 1). A theorem about one transfers to the other only through a closure/limiting statement in the Weil seminorm, and that statement is the cofinal uniform floor (prop:v121-cofinal-rh). Known-equivalent-criterion is not closer-to-RH: Li's criterion is exactly as far from RH as Weil positivity, by construction (it is Weil positivity on a basis).
