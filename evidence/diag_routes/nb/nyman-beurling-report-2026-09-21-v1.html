The Nyman–Beurling / Báez-Duarte route, tested against the certified λ = 4 window

Research-plus-computation report for the manuscript Fixed-Space Prime Compatibility in Burnol's Sonine Quotients (v1.38). Prepared 2026-09-21, version 1. Working directory /tmp/nymanbeurling/; nothing was written into the repository.

Labels used throughout: [exact] closed form or identity; [proved] theorem, hypotheses stated; [numerical] computed here, precision stated; [open].

(a) Bottom line

1. There is no finite Nyman–Beurling statement equivalent to Wλ ≥ 0 at one window, and none of the obvious kind can exist: both objects are unconditional finite computations (dN is an explicit Schur complement of Vasyunin cotangent sums, W4 ≥ 0 is an Arb certificate) and neither is known to constrain the position of any zero; the only equivalences are in the limit (all windows ⇔ RH ⇔ dN → 0). 2. The route's one structural bridge is Burnol's own: the vectors that give the unconditional lower bound dN2 log N ≳ Σρ mρ2/|ρ|2 are the zero-evaluation vectors that span the orthogonal complement of the co-Poisson (arithmetic) subspace in the Sonine space — the same quotient the manuscript calls QR — but Nyman–Beurling asks for the norm of one fixed vector modulo that subspace while Weil positivity asks for the sign of a form on its generators, and nothing transfers at fixed cutoff. 3. Computed to certified precision, dN2 log N sits within a few percent of C = 2 + γ − log 4π = 0.046191… from N = 7 on, but it does not approach C monotonically from above (it is below C at N = 41–48, 220–360, 400–520, 600, sampled, and above C elsewhere up to 600), and the route offers no purchase on the uniformity wall: Burnol's bound proves the best possible rate is C/log N, so a proof would have to hit the sharp constant, with no analogue of the manuscript's "uniform floor suffices" weakening.

1. The criterion and the quantitative results

Setting. K = L2((0,∞), dx), χ = 1(0,1], ρ(x) = {x} the fractional part, ρθ(x) = {θ/x}, ρn = ρ1/n. The Mellin transform ∫0∞ ρθ(x) xs−1 dx = −θs ζ(s)/s for 0 < Re s < 1 (Titchmarsh (2.1.5)) and ∫χ xs−1dx = 1/s are what connect the closure problem to ζ. [exact]

Nyman (1950), Beurling (1955) [proved]: RH ⇔ χ lies in the L2(0,1)-closure of span{ {θ/x} − θ{1/x} : 0 < θ < 1 } (the Beurling combination kills the 1/x tail so the function lives on (0,1]); Bagchi, Proc. Indian Acad. Sci. 116 (2006) 137–146, arXiv:math/0607733, Theorem 5, gives the Hardy-space proof in a page.

Báez-Duarte, Balazard, Landreau, Saias (BBLS), Adv. Math. 149 (2000) 130–144 [proved]: in K, let Bλ = span{ρθ : λ ≤ θ ≤ 1} and D(λ) = dist(χ, Bλ). Then RH ⇔ D(λ) → 0 as λ → 0, and unconditionally liminfλ→0 D(λ)2 log(1/λ) ≥ Σρ 1/|ρ|2 sum over the non-trivial zeros on the critical line, each counted once regardless of multiplicity (if RH fails the left side is +∞ and the bound is trivial).

Burnol, Adv. Math. 170 (2002) 56–70, arXiv:math/0103058, Theorem 1.3 [proved]: liminfλ→0 D(λ)2 log(1/λ) ≥ Σρ mρ2/|ρ|2, zeros counted with the square of their multiplicity. The proof builds vectors Xλρ,k (k < mρ) exactly orthogonal to Bλ, asymptotically orthogonal across distinct zeros as λ → 0, and projects χ onto their span (Burnol's "toy model", Theorem 1.4 there, shows the same m2 law for polynomial approximation on the circle).

Báez-Duarte, Rend. Lincei 14 (2003) 5–11, arXiv:math/0202141, Theorem 1.1 [proved]: RH ⇔ χ lies in the K-closure of span{ρn : n = 1, 2, 3, …}. So with dN := distK(χ, span{ρ1, …, ρN}) = infc ‖χ − Σn≤N cn ρn‖, one has dN non-increasing, D(1/N) ≤ dN (integer dilations are a subset), hence RH ⇔ dN → 0 and, unconditionally, liminf dN2 log N ≥ Σρ mρ2/|ρ|2. On the Mellin side dN2 = infA (1/2π)∫ |1 + ζ(½+it) AN(½+it)|2 dt/(¼+t2) over Dirichlet polynomials AN(s) = Σn≤N cn n−s. (The task statement wrote the closure in L2(0,1); the integer-dilation theorem and every quantitative result are stated in L2(0,∞), and that is what is computed below. The (0,1)-restricted integer problem is not known to be equivalent to RH — the 1/(n(s−1)) tail terms break the annihilation argument — so it is not used.)

The constant. Under RH, |ρ|2 = ρ(1−ρ), so Σρ 1/|ρ|2 = 2 Σρ Re(1/ρ) = 2 + γ − log 4π (zeros counted with multiplicity, Hadamard-product convention; Rosser 1939 as cited by Landreau–Richard). Numerically [exact / numerical] C = 2 + γ − log 4π = 0.046191417932242067628620495812991 , √C = 0.214921887978 . Direct check with the first 2000 zeros (mpmath zetazero, 20 digits) plus the Riemann–von Mangoldt tail 2·(log(T/2π)+1)/(2πT): partial sums 0.039970 (100 zeros), 0.044753 (1000), 0.045307 (2000, γ2000 = 2515.3); with the tail estimate 0.0461982, 0.0461920, 0.0461916 → C. Note what this says about the constant: 59 % of C comes from the first ten zeros, 87 % from zeros below height 237, 98 % from zeros below height 2500. The NB constant is a low-zero quantity.

Conjecture (BBLS 2000; Landreau–Richard, Experiment. Math. 11 (2002) 349–360, Conjectures 1.4 and 3.1) [open]: D(λ)2 log(1/λ) → C and dN2 log N → C. Burnol (2002, and "On Fourier and Zeta(s)", Forum Math. 16 (2004) 789–840, arXiv:math/0112254, §5) points out that by his bound this conjecture implies RH and the simplicity of all zeros; if a multiple zero exists the natural constant is Σ mρ2/|ρ|2 > C.

Gram entries [exact, verified numerically]. With V(h/k) = Σm=1k−1 {mh/k} cot(πm/k) (Vasyunin, Algebra i Analiz 7 (1995) 118–135; in this form Bettin–Conrey, Algebra Number Theory 7 (2013), arXiv:1111.0931, and eq. (1.3) of arXiv:1705.09921), for coprime h, k ⟨ρh, ρk⟩ = (log 2π − γ)/2 · (1/h + 1/k) + (k−h)/(2hk) · log(h/k) − π/(2hk) · (V(h/k) + V(k/h)), ⟨ρm, ρn⟩ = ⟨ρm/g, ρn/g⟩/g for g = gcd(m,n), ⟨ρn, ρn⟩ = (log 2π − γ)/n, ⟨χ, ρn⟩ = (log n + 1 − γ)/n, ‖χ‖ = 1, so dN2 = 1 − bTGN−1b. Verification (verify_formulas.py, mpmath 40 digits): each closed form was compared with an independent quadrature of ∫0∞{t/m}{t/n} dt/t2, folded by periodicity onto [0, lcm(m,n)] with the Hurwitz kernel ζ(2, u/L)/L2, for (m,n) = (1,1), (1,2), (2,3), (3,5), (2,4), (4,6), (5,7), (3,8), and ⟨χ, ρn⟩ for n = 1, 2, 3, 7: all agree to |Δ| ≤ 1.7·10−41. Where the ratio sits. dN decreases at every prime N and barely moves at composite N (Landreau–Richard's observation; here exact). From N = 7 on, dN2 log N / C stays inside [0.9794 (N = 240), 1.1850 (N = 10)]. It is not approaching C from above in any monotone sense: it is below 1 at N = 41–48, 220–360, 400–520, 600 (sampled every 10 up to 200 and every 20 up to 600) and above 1 elsewhere, i.e. it oscillates around C with amplitude of a few percent, spending most of the range 220–520 slightly below C. This is not in tension with Burnol's theorem, which is a liminf statement about the asymptotic constant and says nothing about finite N; but it does say the "1 + o(1)" carries an oscillating correction of relative size ~ ±3 % at N ~ 102–103, and Landreau–Richard's least-squares fit over N ≤ 20 000 (aN ≈ 0.21377 versus √C ≈ 0.21492, i.e. ratio ≈ 0.989) suggests the value is slightly below C at N ~ 104. Reading: the numerics are exactly as consistent with the conjecture dN2 log N → C as Landreau–Richard reported in 2002, and the finite-N oscillation is a second-order term of unknown sign, not a signal about any zero.

3. Is there a finite Nyman–Beurling statement equivalent to Wλ ≥ 0 at one window?

No — and the answer is structural, not a gap in the literature. Both statements are unconditional facts about explicit finite objects. dN2 = 1 − bTGN−1b is an elementary expression in cotangent sums, computable to any precision for any N, whose value is what it is whether or not RH holds. W4 ≥ 0 is likewise a certified statement about one explicit form (the manuscript's Proposition v126-full-window; the classical range λ ≤ √2 is a theorem, Yoshida / Connes–Consani, and Zhu's arXiv:2608.24827 certifies a = 0.8). Neither is known to imply anything about the location of a single zero, so neither can imply a non-trivial statement about the other. The equivalences are all limiting: {Wλ ≥ 0 for every λ} ⇔ RH ⇔ {dN → 0}, and, per the manuscript's v1.36 reduction, {Wλ ≥ −C*·I along a cofinal family} ⇔ RH. A transfer of the form "Wλ ≥ 0 for all λ ≤ Λ ⇒ dN ≤ ε(Λ) with ε → 0" would be a new theorem deducing quantitative approximation from finite-window positivity; nothing of the kind is in the literature (searched: Burnol 2001–2004, Báez-Duarte 2002–2005, Bagchi 2006, Balazard's surveys, Bettin–Conrey 2013, Ehm arXiv:2405.06349, Bellemare–Langlois–Ransford arXiv:2011.02847), and the heuristic below says why it should not be expected.

The bridge that does exist (Burnol). Take Ka (de Branges' Sonine space: f ∈ K with f and its cosine transform vanishing on (0,a)) or its variant La (both constant instead of vanishing on (0,a)), 0 < a < 1. Burnol, J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 16 (2004) 65–94, arXiv:math/0203120, Theorem 3.1 [proved]: the evaluation vectors Yaρ,k (0 ≤ k < mρ, ⟨f, Yaρ,k⟩ = the k-th derivative of the completed Mellin transform of f at ρ) form a minimal system in La iff a ≤ 1, a complete system iff a ≥ 1, and for a < 1 the orthogonal complement of their closed span is exactly the co-Poisson subspace Pa (the square-integrable co-Poisson sums of functions supported in [a, 1/a]). Theorem 3.3 there: the functions ζ(s)/(s−ρ)l, 1 ≤ l ≤ mρ, are the dual system at a = 1. In "On Fourier and Zeta(s)" §5 Burnol states explicitly that the Yλs,k which realize ζ and its derivatives on the critical line as scalar products are, for ζ(ρ) = 0 and k < mρ, exactly perpendicular to ρ1 and all its contractions ρθ, λ ≤ θ ≤ 1 — i.e. to Bλ — and that this is how the 2002 lower bound is obtained; the quotients HPλ = Kλ/W'λ are what he proposes as approximate Hilbert–Pólya spaces. So the NB quotient K/closure(Bλ) and the Sonine quotient La/Pa — the manuscript's QR = HR ⊖ WR, with its zero evaluators vpRρ — are spanned by the same family of zero-evaluation vectors. The scales match as the task guessed: the NB problem at N uses Dirichlet polynomials with frequencies log n ∈ [0, log N] and involves primes ≤ N; the Weil window [1/λ, λ] uses test-function frequencies in [−log λ, log λ], total length 2 log λ, and its prime sum runs to λ2; hence N ≈ λ2, i.e. N = 16 at λ = 4.

Why the shared quotient does not transfer. Nyman–Beurling asks for the norm of one fixed vector, χ (Mellin transform 1/s), modulo the arithmetic subspace: dN2 is the squared length of the image of χ in the quotient, and Burnol's bound says each zero holds a fixed share ≈ mρ2/(|ρ|2 log N) of it. Weil positivity asks whether a quadratic form on the generators of the arithmetic subspace — under RH, Σρ |F(ρ)|2 for F the Mellin transform of the test function, and unconditionally the explicit-formula expression — is nonnegative. The first is a coordinate of a specific vector; the second is the signature of a Gram-type form. If RH fails, the NB limit is lim dN2 = 1 − ΠRe ρ > ½ |(1−ρ)/ρ|2 (Burnol's Blaschke-product formula for the projection of 1 onto the Nyman space, quoted from "On Fourier and Zeta(s)" §5; not re-derived here) — for a single off-line zero at σ + iγ this is (2σ−1)/(σ2+γ2), arbitrarily small at large height — while the Weil form acquires an indefinite pair term F(ρ)F(1−ρ) + conjugate. Both objects are dominated by low zeros (98 % of C from height < 2500), but that is a property of the weight 1/|ρ|2, not a link between them at finite cutoff.

Heuristic for why no finite-window transfer should be expected [heuristic, not proved]. A test function of support half-width a resolves the critical line at scale 1/a in t. Around height T the zeros have density (log T)/2π; a window functional that tried to isolate one off-line zero ρ0 = ½ + δ + iγ would capture ~ (log T)/(2πa) neighbouring zeros, each contributing positively ~ |F|2, against an indefinite term of size ~ sinh2(δa)|F|2 from ρ0. At a = log 4 = 1.386, sinh2(δa) ≤ sinh2(0.69) = 0.56 even at the edge δ = ½, which is below the density penalty already at T ~ 100. So W4 ≥ 0 constrains nothing inside the strip — which is exactly the uniformity wall the manuscript records — and a fortiori bounds no dN.

Numerical check at N ≈ λ2 = 16 [numerical, certified]. d16 = 0.13376854442270587516…, d162 = 0.017894023476969435099…, d162 log 16 = 0.0496127676877393 = 1.0741 · C. This number is a theorem about sixteen cotangent sums; it would be identical if ζ had off-line zeros, and W4 ≥ 0 neither raises nor lowers it. Conversely, nothing about d16 (or any dN) feeds back into the sign of W4: the NB Gram matrix (GN with χ adjoined) is positive definite for every N, trivially, since it is a Gram matrix of independent L2 functions.

4. Purchase on the uniformity wall?

What a proof would have to establish. RH ⇔ dN → 0, i.e. an upper bound: Dirichlet polynomials AN with (1/2π)∫ |1 + ζ(½+it)AN(½+it)|2 dt/(¼+t2) → 0. The weight 1/|s|2 puts most of the mass at |t| = O(1), where ζ is an explicit fixed function; so the statement is that mollifier-type polynomials (the natural candidates are Möbius weights, cn = μ(n)(1 − log n/log N) and refinements — Báez-Duarte's fε, the Selberg approximation Sn in arXiv:math/0202141 §1) converge to −1/ζ in weighted mean on the line, uniformly as N → ∞, in the regime mollifier length ≫ height. That is Littlewood's criterion (Σ μ(n)n−s → 1/ζ(s) for Re s > ½ ⇔ RH) pushed onto the line in L2, and it is the regime in which mollifier mean-value theorems (which need N ≤ Tθ) say nothing. The natural sequences are known to be bad: Fn = Σa≤n μ(a)ρa diverges in K (Báez-Duarte 2002, citing his earlier work), and the optimal coefficients found here have no visible closed form.

Burnol's bound turns the target into a sharp constant. Since unconditionally dN2 ≥ (1 + o(1)) Σρ mρ2/(|ρ|2 log N), the approximation cannot beat C/log N; the conjecture says it attains it. A proof of RH by this route therefore has to show the residual energy of χ transverse to the arithmetic span is O(1/log N) with the right constant — there is no slack of the form "prove dN → 0 at any rate", because the only rate available is the one the zeros dictate, and every zero direction retains a fixed share ~ mρ2/(|ρ|2 log N) of it. This is the uniformity wall in NB clothing: control, uniform in the cutoff N, of infinitely many zero directions whose density grows like log T.

Is it easier than the Weil route? No; on two counts it is worse. (i) The Weil side has the manuscript's v1.36 weakening — a uniform finite floor Wλ ≥ −C*·I along a cofinal family already implies RH — and v1.38 shows floors −8·I are cheap to certify at λ = 5, 6, 8. Nyman–Beurling has no analogue: dN2 ≤ 1 − ε is vacuous, because by the Blaschke formula an off-line zero at height γ makes lim dN2 ≈ (2σ−1)/γ2, as small as one likes. The NB target is the full limit, and its rate. (ii) What NB does offer is one monotone scalar sequence with a proved lower-bound rate and a conjectured exact asymptotic, which makes it a superb diagnostic (the table above is consistent with C to ±5 % from N = 7) but a poor proof vehicle: the finite values are elementary cotangent identities carrying no information about zeros, exactly as the finite window certificates are. The one place where NB is genuinely more explicit — Burnol's per-zero decomposition of the defect — is also where its wall is most visible: to prove dN → 0 one must show χ has no component along any Yρ,k with Re ρ ≠ ½, and the only handle on those directions is the Hardy-space/Blaschke argument that already proves the qualitative criterion without touching the critical-line zeros (Burnol's own remark: "a disappointing fact that this theorem can be proven without leading to any new information whatsoever on the zeros lying on the critical line").

What the route does not offer. No rate-free reformulation, no finite certificate that carries arithmetic content, no floor-type weakening, and no known way to make the mollifier length exceed the height. Same wall, sharper constant.

(d) Next step

No live angle on RH from this route. Two small, honest uses of what was learned: (1) In the manuscript's discussion of the arithmetic quotient QR and its zero evaluators, cite Burnol's Theorem 3.1 (arXiv:math/0203120) as the published statement that the co-Poisson subspace's orthogonal complement in La is the closed span of the zero evaluators for a < 1, and note in one sentence that the Nyman–Beurling lower bound is the norm of χ in that same quotient — a semantic lock with the literature, not a result. (2) If a diagnostic is wanted, the cheapest informative computation is Burnol's per-zero decomposition of dN2 at N ≤ 200: Gram matrix of the first K zero vectors X1/Nρ,0 against χ, to see how much of the certified dN2 the first ten zeros (59 % of C) account for at finite N. It would sharpen the conjecture's second-order term; it would not move RH.
