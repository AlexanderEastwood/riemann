1. Kernel exclusion: the primary route

For a function in the complete logarithmic form domain, being a nullvector is exactly equivalent to its screw potential being affine inside the window. This uses a continuous integral equation and keeps the boundary contribution of zero extension. It does not assume a square-integrable derivative.

A rank-one perturbation of the same logarithmic principal operator supplies a rigorous countermodel to the generic derivative-regularity shortcut. This is a failure of that inference, not a counterexample to the arithmetic operator.

Open input: affine-potential injectivity, API. The actual arithmetic equation must have only the zero solution at every finite window. With the established continuity theorem, this would prevent the first sign crossing even while positive spectral gaps approach zero.

2. Heat deformation: cancellation survives, a proposed comparison may not

Counting a finite zero cluster with multiplicities removes the inverse-gap singularities of individual zero velocities. The correct heat coefficient in Xi coordinates is 1/4. The exact derivative still contains interaction with zeros outside the selected cluster.

The elementary estimate grows with support. There is also a precise conditional obstruction: if the real-zero deformed function has one zero absent from the original Xi zero set, a uniform comparison using the same tests is impossible. This checkpoint does not prove or certify that unmatched-zero hypothesis at a chosen time.

3. Arithmetic approximation: a surviving gain bound

Adding integer dilations gives the exact residualized block improvement hᵀS⁻¹h, where S is the full Schur complement. An unconditional trace estimate bounds this below by ||h||²/[κ log(M/N)], with κ = log(2π) − γ. The old/new interactions remain in h and S.

Open input: a sufficient lower estimate for the corrected arithmetic correlations, named RBC. No such asymptotic estimate is proved here. Any rigorous approximation rate tending to zero would suffice; the optimal rate is not required. The exact finite formula alone does not establish convergence.
