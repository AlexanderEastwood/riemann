Prime shifts do not yet give an injectivity mechanism

The exact prime-shift equation is two-sided. Two tempting shortcuts fail for concrete reasons: the full semigroup does not preserve nonnegative functions, and prime entry is not an analytic perturbation of the raw bounded operator.

These are scoped findings. They produce no arithmetic nullvector, no uniform lower bound, and no proof of RH or G2.

The equation that must be solved

Let H be the clamped antiderivative of a candidate nullvector f, and let M be its total integral. Each prime-power ramp contributes exactly

wn[H(x − log n) + H(x + log n) − M].

The remaining term retains the logarithmic archimedean interaction and both pole terms. This formula is valid at the complete form-domain regularity; it does not assume f has a square-integrable derivative.

It is not a one-way recursion. Both shifted sides appear, and the remaining nonlocal term still depends on f throughout the interval. Vanishing on a small interval does not make that term vanish. A separate prime-free exterior condition may provide additional information; it is not assumed here.

A genuine obstruction to a positive-transfer argument

Away from prime-power separations, the full off-diagonal kernel is J(t) = 2 cosh(t/2) − ρ(t), with ρ(t) = et/2/(et − e−t). Its sign is the sign of u³ − u − 1, where u = et.

Let r > 1 solve r³ − r − 1 = 0. On every interval of length L > log r, two disjoint nonnegative smooth bumps can be placed where their mixed Weil energy is strictly positive. The corresponding semigroup pairing is then negative for sufficiently small positive time.

Proved: the complete full-space semigroup does not preserve the ordinary cone of nonnegative functions. A bounded positive change of weight, with bounded inverse, cannot repair that property.

This is not negative diagonal Weil energy. It does not exclude a different cone, a specialized uniqueness theorem, or positivity of a particular eigenfunction. No separate parity-restricted order claim is made.

Prime entry and analytic Fredholm theory

A compressed prime shift is zero before its entry threshold and has operator norm 1 immediately after entry. The actual bounded arithmetic perturbation therefore has a norm jump there. Integrating to the screw potential softens the entry: its exact Hilbert–Schmidt size is w(L − ℓ)+²/√6. It still is not analytic across entry.

At a fixed window there is a valid alternative: choose a positive logarithmic reference operator T and form C = T−1/2BT−1/2. The nullvector question is exactly whether −1 is an eigenvalue of this compact self-adjoint operator. Coupling-parameter Fredholm theory makes the exceptional parameters discrete; it does not show that the physical parameter avoids them.

What remains missing

We still need arithmetic uniqueness for the complete shifted-primitive equation, or a proof excluding the physical exceptional eigenvalue. Neither follows from the identities or from discreteness alone. The finding closes the stated shortcut inferences; it does not close the injectivity route.

The proof uses the archived complete operator and affine-potential identities. Relevant primary frameworks were checked against Chen–Weth’s logarithmic Laplacian paper and Taylor’s analytic Fredholm paper.
