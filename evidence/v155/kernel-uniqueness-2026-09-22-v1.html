Local geometry does not yet give arithmetic uniqueness

We proved that two tempting shortcuts fail for the actual arithmetic operator: vanishing on a local patch does not force global vanishing, and its semigroup does not preserve pointwise positivity. The whole-window kernel question remains open.

These are exact analytical results. They do not exhibit a full-window nullvector, a negative Weil direction, or a uniform lower bound. G2 and RH remain open.

The construction that changes the local argument

Inside any interval with half-width a > (log 2)/2, choose three small, separated patches. Put an arbitrary smooth function on the middle patch W. Choose the values on V so that the prime-2 shift exactly cancels the integral contribution on U. The resulting nonzero function satisfies both f = 0 and Aaf = 0 on U.

V is U shifted left by log 2. All other prime shifts miss these patches. A convergent Neumann inverse makes the cancellation exact. The equation is imposed on U, not on the whole interval.

The witnesses belong to the complete operator domain. A symmetric construction works in each parity for a > log 2. Its infinite-dimensional family allows finitely many linear constraints, including the actual source orthogonality, without eliminating every witness.

This disproves a proposed local property; it is not merely a failed numerical bound. It also explains why a theorem for the logarithmic Laplacian plus a local potential cannot be transferred by treating the prime shifts as a harmless perturbation.

The supporting notes also show why the raw bounded prime perturbation is not analytic at a newly entering prime shift. A valid compact Fredholm reduction exists at a fixed window, but a discrete exceptional set does not exclude the physical coupling from that set.

What the geometry represents

The logarithmic principal operator has a known harmonic extension in three dimensions. Its boundary formula contains a renormalized trace. The arithmetic equation leaves a nonlocal prime-and-pole term in that trace; drawing or lifting the operator into a higher-dimensional space does not determine its sign. The needed theorem remains global arithmetic injectivity, not local vanishing alone.

The relevant primary sources are Chen–Hauer–Weth, extension and unique continuation, Harrach–Lin–Weth, the logarithmic Schrödinger equation, and Suzuki, the complete Weil operator. The new scoped statements above are proved directly in the accompanying evidence; no worldwide novelty is asserted.
