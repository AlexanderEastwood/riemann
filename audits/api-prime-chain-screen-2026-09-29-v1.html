Positive prime-power chains do not exclude a first zero

The proposed API shortcut loses the arithmetic information it needs. A finite change to the coefficient at n=2 preserves every original prime-chain lower and upper operator bound, while NS57 supplies a nonzero nullvector at a finite first-zero window for that altered family. This is a scope-matched application of an existing control, not a new impossibility theorem for API.

Wall check: Known wall for the bounds-only inference. Closest result: NS57’s finite-weight first-zero controls. The retained hypotheses all survive the change. Same open gap remains for original KERNEL-API (NS51/55/58): the exact Euler coefficient recurrence and original radical identities are not preserved by this control.

The proposed arithmetic input

For each prime p, put r=p−1/2. The exact original weights wpᵏ=log(p)rk give

Pp(θ)=1+2Σk≥1rkcos(kθ)
=(1−r²)/(1−2r cosθ+r²).

This classical Poisson-kernel identity yields the bounds mp≤Pp(θ)≤Mp, where mp=(1−r)/(1+r) and Mp=1/mp.

Let Tℓ be the unitary translation by ℓ=log p on the whole line. The complete chain is the norm-convergent bounded operator

Cp=I+Σk≥1rk(Tkℓ+T−kℓ),
mpI ≤ Cp ≤ MpI.

The same bounds hold after compression to any finite window and in either parity. For a compactly supported f, only finitely many correlations in this complete series are nonzero. Its exact contribution to the prime part of the Weil form is log(p)(‖f‖²−⟨Cpf,f⟩). No infinite sum over primes is interchanged here.

Candidate screened: retaining these bounds for every prime, positive weights, the original prime locations, gamma and pole terms, and nonnegativity of the full form at a first-zero window would exclude a nonzero nullvector. The candidate uses the bounds derived from the Euler recurrence; it does not retain that exact recurrence as an additional hypothesis.

A matched control preserves even the original bounds

Choose ε=1/100 and change only

w2 → w2−ε log 2 = log 2(1/√2−ε).

This coefficient remains positive. All other coefficients, all locations, the archimedean term and both pole terms remain unchanged. The modified 2-chain symbol is

P̃2(θ)=P2(θ)−2ε cosθ.

The following elementary bound covers all frequencies; it does not extrapolate the numerical grid. Write c=cosθ. P2 is increasing in c, with P2(0)=1/3 and endpoint values m2=3−2√2, M2=3+2√2.

  • If c≥0, then 47/150=1/3−1/50≤P̃2≤M2. Also 47/150>1/5>m2, since √2>7/5.
  • If c≤0, then m2≤P̃2≤1/3+1/50=53/150<3<M2.

Thus the original constants m2, M2 still bound the modified chain. Its constant Fourier coefficient is still one. The other prime chains are exactly unchanged. Fourier multiplication and compression transfer the bounds to every full-line or finite-window test, including both parities.

Where the existing control applies

The perturbation is precisely NS57 with δ2=−ε log 2 and every other δn=0. Consequently the retained chain bounds do not imply the candidate’s kernel exclusion. This deduction uses the archived NS57 theorem and its stated complete-domain and near-radical dependencies.
