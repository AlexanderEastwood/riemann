One arithmetic correction fails the finite descent test

The first concrete attempt to turn the optimized coefficients into an improvement across several scales does not give a reliable full step. It increases the squared error at two of the three sizes checked. We retain an exact arithmetic identity and a complete tail estimate, but have no new asymptotic gain bound. The manuscript remains v1.66; RH and G2 remain open.

What was tried

Starting with the actual best coefficients at size N, we formed a nonlinear correction through N². Its formula combines coefficients according to integer factorizations. The resulting divisor defect is exactly the Dirichlet convolution square of the old defect throughout that range. This means summing products over divisors; it does not mean squaring the error point by point.

The calculation measures the complete squared error, including the exterior and the entire remaining tail. It also measures the best step length in this direction, and then allows all old coefficients to be refitted. These modifications generally sacrifice the exact convolution-square identity.

What remains useful

The optimized residual now has an exact integral-versus-lattice-sum description. Define a potential P by integrating the residual twice in the appropriate logarithmic coordinates. Its value P(1) is exactly the optimal squared error. At every old integer dilation, the complete difference between the integral of P and its lattice sum vanishes.

We proved a complete bound for the part of each observation beyond a finite cutoff. We also found a shared tail term that cancels exactly before numerical evaluation; retaining that cancellation improves the certificate. The signed finite sum still needs an arithmetic estimate from below. Neither identity supplies it.

This operator belongs to the classical Müntz / Nyman–Beurling framework. A hypothetical off-critical zero would remain invisible to all the corresponding complete observations. Rewriting the equations does not exclude that obstruction.
