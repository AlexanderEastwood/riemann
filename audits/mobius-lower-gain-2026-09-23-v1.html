The lower-gain attempt still needs signed cancellation

No cofinal lower bound was obtained. This work tests a concrete estimate on the route in PR42. It does not change the manuscript version or claim progress on the missing theorem beyond diagnosing this estimate.

We combined the actual optimized observations using Möbius coefficients and a logarithmic taper. Those coefficients had already been tested numerically in NS73. The new work asks whether their positive finite gain has an analytic lower-bound explanation.

The exact algebra cancels the prime main terms when the old normal equations are used. What remains is a joint signed expression: the normalization term, the full finite divisor sum, and the complete outer tail.

gain numerator = (log 2) × current error − joint signed remainder

At size 256, evaluating this expression with all signs retained recovers the positive numerator. But the straightforward lower bound obtained by replacing each term with its absolute value is below −186 times the current error. Doubling the physical cutoff makes that bound worse, below −226 times the error. A negative lower bound cannot force improvement.

There is an exact explanation for this loss: at any fixed size with a nonzero logarithmic or constant residual tail, the absolute divisor allowance grows without limit as the cutoff increases, while the signed expression converges. The proof uses an explicit arithmetic progression of nonzero divisor coefficients. It does not assume RH.

This statement fixes the size before increasing the cutoff. It does not exclude a bound at PR42’s coupled cutoff N6, a different signed estimate, or the full joint observation method. The finite cutoffs tested here are not N6.
