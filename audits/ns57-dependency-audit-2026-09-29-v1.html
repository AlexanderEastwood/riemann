What “first zero” means here

It is the first window where the altered operator’s lowest energy reaches zero. It is not a newly located zero of the zeta function. At that full first-zero window the complete altered form is nonnegative and has a nonzero nullvector.

The explicit alteration is δ2=−log(2)/100, with every other coefficient unchanged. Its n=2 weight stays positive. 1. A negative perturbation direction becomes a negative compact test

Write d=log(2)/100 and ℓ=log 2. The bounded perturbation is B=d(Tℓ+T−ℓ), whose Fourier multiplier is 2d cos(ℓξ). Paired Fourier bumps where cos(ℓξ)≤−1/2 give normalized real even and real odd vectors with B-energy at most −d.

Ordinary L² density of the finite radical span then supplies one fixed approximation h to each vector. Accuracy better than 1/16 preserves a strict negative margin because B is bounded. This approximation, including all its coefficients and centers, is fixed before the window grows.

For the smooth cutoffs ga=χah, the complete original operator residual tends to zero and ga tends to h in ordinary norm. Thus

qδ,a[ga]=qa[ga]+⟨Bga,ga⟩ → ⟨Bh,h⟩<0.

The audit traced the complete residual: it retains prime rows beyond the finite window cutoff and both pole terms. The smooth compact tests belong to the actual operator domain. No uniform approximation coefficients, global form-norm density, positive gap or RH assumption is used.

3. Compactness resolves the continuity question

After scaling to (−1,1), all windows have one logarithmic form domain. The form is t[u]+⟨K(a)u,u⟩, where t measures the log-weighted Fourier energy and K(a) is locally bounded and strongly continuous.

At a prime-entry point, the compressed translation is strongly continuous but need not be continuous in operator norm. The argument does not assume otherwise. Bounded t-energy controls the Fourier tails; fixed physical support then makes these sets compact in L². Minimizing vectors therefore have strongly convergent subsequences. This compactness, together with the bounded strong continuity of K(a), makes the K(a) quadratic values converge. Lower semicontinuity of t gives the lower limit inequality, and a fixed minimizing test gives the upper limit inequality, proving continuity of the lowest energy.

The principal multiplier is continuous in norm under dilation; the audited bound is supξ|α(ξ/a)−α(ξ/b)|≤5|log(a/b)|. Both signed pole terms are retained as a continuous finite-rank operator. The standard digamma asymptotic used to identify the logarithmic domain is recorded in DLMF 5.11.2.

4. The zero is attained, with the stated support

Continuity between an initially positive window and a window containing a negative compact test gives a finite first zero. Compactness gives a minimizing vector, and the closed-form representation puts it in the operator domain as a genuine nullvector.

If a full first-zero nullvector had smaller support diameter, translating it into a smaller window would preserve its norm and complete form value, contradicting positivity there. The pole pair is kept together to justify translation invariance. For a parity-sector first zero, symmetry gives the same contradiction in a smaller centered interval, without translating out of the sector.

Scope: the full first-zero parameter is the smaller of the two parity parameters. At a sector’s own first zero, only that sector is guaranteed nonnegative; the other may already be negative. Reaching both endpoints refers to essential support, not to a nonzero boundary trace. No simplicity or numerical threshold is asserted.
