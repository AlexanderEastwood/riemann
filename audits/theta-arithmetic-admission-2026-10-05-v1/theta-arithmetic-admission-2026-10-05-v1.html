The test that became concrete

Take the two shifted original theta factors together. Their total mass is M; their second moment is C. Their quotient k = C/M measures the spread of their product.

M(t) = integral phi(s+t)*phi(s-t) ds
C(t) = integral s^2*phi(s+t)*phi(s-t) ds
k(t) = C(t)/M(t)

We supplied an explicit correction, integrating from minus infinity and centering by k, that keeps both boundary terms zero. If k were a positive-definite kernel, this construction would establish the complete first-Laguerre inequality. Positive definiteness means that every finite matrix built from the kernel has a nonnegative quadratic form; ordinary positivity of k is insufficient.

The proposed estimating condition was specific: represent k as a nonnegative mixture of powers of sech(2t). Each such power has the required positive-definite property. This gave a definite sufficient conjecture with a complete transfer to the target.

The original tail rejects that mixture

After a change of variable, the proposed mixture would make Q a nonnegative mixture of decaying exponentials. The complete original theta expansion instead gives:

Q(z) = k(arcosh(exp(z))/2)

Q(z) = exp(-z)/(16*pi)
     - exp(-2*z)/(128*pi^2)
     + O(exp(-3*z))

The leading coefficient fixes an atom in any proposed positive mixture. All its remaining contributions would be nonnegative, so it must approach that leading term from above. The original theta quotient approaches from below. The negative second correction rules out this precise mixture class.

The derivation retains the original polynomial factors, every other lattice term and the outer integration tails. Independent reviewers reproduced both coefficients. No onset height or numerical certificate is asserted.

Scope: this does not show that k fails positive definiteness. It does not show that the original first-Laguerre expression is negative. Other nonlocal corrections remain unexcluded. PR77’s positive Gaussian-mixture rejection concerned C itself; it did not already settle this different quotient and mixture.

The two arithmetic attempts

Divisor variance. Grouping the two zeta factors by their product gives explicit coefficients involving prime-factor exponents and squared logarithms. These coefficients are nonnegative in their convergent Dirichlet series. On the critical line, however, the full completion and oscillatory contributions remain. No positive main term and compatible lower remainder estimate were obtained.

Riemann–Siegel sums. We wrote the complete quadratic with both ratio and product phases, gamma curvature and every differentiated remainder term. At the balanced cutoff, the stationary dual sum has comparable length. The tested absolute-value budget grows like N, while its positive diagonal grows only like (log N)^3. This explains the failure of that proposed bound; it does not determine the actual oscillatory sign or exclude all Poisson methods.

What is still missing

J(r) = (r^2+1/4)^2*(Y'^2-Y*Y'')
     + 2*(r^2-1/4)*Y^2

Required: J(r) >= 0 for every real r.

The full correction, zeros and mixed terms are included. A proof of this first inequality alone would still not prove RH; the distinction is explicit in Csordas’s formulation of the Laguerre inequalities.

The NS101 control shares the generic centering identities. In its established negative-Laguerre range, its own quotient cannot be positive definite. Thus “it is a conditional variance” supplies no sign theorem. The control changes the original lattice and nonlinear initial data; that mismatch remains a distinction, not a successful screen.

Decision: stop these three attempts. A future conjecture need not already be proved to deserve review. It must supply a concrete estimator and its complete conditional link to the target. Changing the mixture or running a larger finite table is not an automatic next step.
