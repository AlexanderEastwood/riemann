## 1. The nonlocal proposal qualifies as a concrete question, then fails

The proposal keeps the complete original kernel and defines

```text
w(s,t)=phi(s+t)*phi(s-t),
M(t)=int_R w(s,t) ds,
C(t)=int_R s^2*w(s,t) ds,
k(t)=C(t)/M(t),
V(s,t)=int_(-infinity)^s [a^2-k(t)]*w(a,t) da.
```

Its correction `h=V/w` is explicit. Since `M>0`, no transform or zero is divided out. Differentiating `V` gives the stated residual `k((u-v)/2)`; centering makes both complete flux boundaries vanish. Alternatively, the transfer needs no correction calculation: `M` is an autocorrelation and therefore positive definite. If `k` is positive definite, their pointwise product `C=M*k` is positive definite, so its integrable Fourier transform is nonnegative. PR76's exact normalization then gives `J=16*L1[X]>=0` at every real height.

This is more specific than PR77's request for an unspecified nonlocal correction. Its arithmetic conjecture was an exact positive mixture

```text
k(t)=int_[0,infinity) sech(2t)^a dnu(a), nu finite and nonnegative.
```

It has the five required features: an exact expression; fixed original coefficients; every real t; a proposed positive-Laplace estimating technique for the transformed quotient; and a complete implication to the first-Laguerre target without any extra sign lemma. The Fourier integral of each positive power follows directly from the beta integral and is positive. It was therefore a meaningful paper feasibility test. Describing it merely as having no method would undersell what was actually specified.

The original-kernel asymptotic rejects this particular estimator. With `A=2*pi*exp(2t)`, the complete dominant-pair calculation gives

```text

Q(z)=k(arcosh(exp(z))/2)
    =exp(-z)/(16*pi)-exp(-2z)/(128*pi^2)+O(exp(-3z)).
```

I checked the decisive relative coefficients: the zeroth Gaussian moment has correction `-6-3/24=-49/8`; the second has `-6-15/24=-53/8`. Their ratio gives the displayed negative second coefficient. The uniform original-series remainder and the outside-neighborhood tail are retained in note 01; no full-line termwise theta unfolding is used.

If `Q(z)=int exp(-az)dnu(a)` with positive finite nu, its finite nonzero limit after multiplication by exp(z) forces no mass below 1 and an atom of mass `1/(16*pi)` at 1. Hence `exp(z)Q(z)` approaches that mass from above. The displayed expansion approaches it from below. The contradiction needs no computed threshold.

