1. Executive Summary: Resolving the Two-Sided Overlap Obstacle

As the number of primes K in the Euler-factor preconditioner L𝒫K increases, an increasing number of reduced-fraction combination shifts τr = log r fall into the two-sided overlap regime 0 < τr < b (where both x + τr and x − τr remain inside [−b, b] for |x| < b − τr). Previously, single-reflection step minorants applied only to shifts in [b, 2b), forcing all two-sided shifts τr ∈ (0, b) into the pure-diagonal Schur penalty Va(x) — turning positive stabilizing shifts (cr > 0) into double negative penalties (−2cr) on [0, b − τr].

In Theorem 1, we prove that for every positive shift cr > 0 across both regimes τr ∈ (0, 2b), the positive semidefinite Schur operator Mτr+ maps the constant function 1[−b, b] to 2crψr(x), where ψr(x) = 1[0, b−τr](x) + 1[τr−b, b](x) lies inside the existing step subspace 𝒮step with zero additional cuts. Summing over all positive shifts and applying operator Cauchy–Schwarz yields the closed-form Sherman–Morrison trace reduction:

Tr(Ksherman, even) = Tr(Kpure, even) − [ (2 / Spos) Ipos ] / [ 1 + (2 / Spos) Jpos ],

which achieves zero loss on the constant mode 1[0, b] and preserves the exact block-invariant step-subspace decomposition P0Πstep = ΠstepP0.
