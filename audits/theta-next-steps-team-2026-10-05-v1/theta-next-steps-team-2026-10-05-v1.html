Why “just a lower bound” is misleading

The identity and boundary repair tell us what to estimate. They do not supply an estimating mechanism. The new reviews separate three things that had been easy to conflate:

  • Accuracy is not a sign margin. A small approximation error only proves positivity if the same comparison has a lower margin large enough to absorb it.
  • A distinguishing identity is not an inequality. The original Jacobi evolution differs from the NS101 control, but uniqueness of that evolution does not bound its signed transform.
  • This first target is not RH by itself. Proving the first Laguerre inequality would settle that partial target. An additional real-zero argument would still be needed.

For an elementary scope example, F(r) = 1 − r^4 satisfies F′² − F·F″ = 4r^6 + 12r² ≥ 0 on the real line, yet it has the nonreal zeros +i and −i. This polynomial is not an original-theta control; it shows why the single inequality alone is insufficient in general.

What the reviews change

They reject a few specific constructions and sharpen the admission requirements. The original slope’s divided-difference matrix fails a two-point test in its large-coordinate tail. Direct half-line convexity and positive Gaussian scale mixtures also cannot represent the complete associated kernel. These are narrow failures, not a closure of all geometric or kernel methods.

The prime-side review retains the exact residues in the Gonek–Hughes–Keating smoothing formula. Distant residues can be bounded; the local signed contribution remains. Its hypothetical off-line pair calculation tests the proposed error mechanism, not the zeros of the actual zeta function.

The Bessel review also finds a concrete mismatch of scales: the available crude lower allowance is of order exp(−r²)/r². Merely beating an exponential in |r| does not meet that allowance. A larger cutoff could improve the approximation error, but would still leave the comparison to the reference function unestimated.
