## 1. Theorem 1: Block-Invariant Step-Subspace Decomposition & $d$-Mode Cauchy–Poincaré Interlacing (`KERNEL-API`)

### 1.1 Orthogonal Step-Subspace Decomposition and Commutation with $P_0$

Let $0 = y_0 < y_1 < \dots < y_{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} = b$ be the elementary partition of $[0, b]$ generated by all shift breakpoints and step-vector endpoints, with elementary intervals $I_k = (y_{k-1}, y_k)$ of lengths $\ell_k = y_k - y_{k-1} > 0$. Decompose $L^2(0, b)$ into the orthogonal direct sum
$$L^2(0, b) = \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}} \oplus \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp, \qquad \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}} := \bigoplus_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} \mathbb{R}\,\mathbf{1}_{I_k}, \qquad \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} L^2_0(I_k),$$
where $L^2_0(I_k) := \left\{g \in L^2(I_k) : \int_{I_k} g(x)\,dx = 0\right\}$, and let $\Pi_{\mathrm{step}}$ be the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$.

**Theorem 1 (Block-Invariant Step-Subspace Decomposition & $d$-Mode Cauchy–Poincaré Interlacing).**  
*Suppose $P_0 = V(x) + \sum_{m=1}^{M_{\mathrm{tot}}} b_m^{-1} |w_m\rangle\langle w_m|$ on $L^2(0, b)$, where $V(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} v_k \mathbf{1}_{I_k}(x)$ with $v_k > 0$, $b_m > 0$, and every step vector $w_m \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$ is piecewise constant on $\{I_k\}_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}}$. Let $A_-^{(\Delta_\alpha)} \succeq 0$ be the low-frequency archimedean operator on $L^2(0, b)$ and $K := P_0^{-1/2} A_-^{(\Delta_\alpha)} P_0^{-1/2}$. Then:*
1. **(Exact Block-Diagonalization of $P_0$ and $P_0^{-1/2}$):** *$P_0$ leaves both $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$ and $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp$ invariant ($P_0 \Pi_{\mathrm{step}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{step}} P_0$), and acts on $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp$ purely as diagonal multiplication by $V(x)$:*
   $$P_0\big|_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp} = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} v_k I_{L^2_0(I_k)}, \qquad P_0^{-1/2}\big|_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp} = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} v_k^{-1/2} I_{L^2_0(I_k)}.$$
   *In the step basis $\{\mathbf{1}_{I_k}\}_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}}$, the quadratic form of $P_0$ on $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$ has the exact $N_{\mathrm{elem}} \times N_{\mathrm{elem}}$ symmetric positive-definite matrix*
   $$(M_{\mathrm{step}})_{kl} := \langle \mathbf{1}_{I_k}, P_0 \mathbf{1}_{I_l} \rangle_{L^2(0, b)} = \ell_k v_k \delta_{kl} + \sum_{m=1}^{M_{\mathrm{tot}}} \frac{1}{b_m} (\ell_k W_{km})(\ell_l W_{lm}).$$
2. **($d$-Mode Cauchy–Poincaré Step-Subspace Interlacing Bound):** *Let $q_1, \dots, q_d \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$ ($1 \le d \le N_{\mathrm{elem}}$) be any $d$ step functions that are orthonormal with respect to $P_0$ ($\langle q_i, P_0 q_j \rangle_{L^2} = q_i^T M_{\mathrm{step}} q_j = \delta_{ij}$), and let $G^{(d)}_{ij} := \langle q_i, A_-^{(\Delta_\alpha)} q_j \rangle_{L^2}$ be their $d \times d$ archimedean Gram matrix with eigenvalues $\mu_1(G^{(d)}) \ge \mu_2(G^{(d)}) \ge \dots \ge \mu_d(G^{(d)}) \ge 0$. Then the eigenvalues $\lambda_1(K) \ge \lambda_2(K) \ge \dots \ge 0$ of $K$ satisfy $\lambda_j(K) \ge \mu_j(G^{(d)})$ for all $1 \le j \le d$, and consequently*
   $$\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \lambda_1(K) \le \operatorname{Tr}(K) - \sum_{j=2}^d \mu_j(G^{(d)}) = \operatorname{Tr}(K) - \operatorname{Tr}(G^{(d)}) + \mu_1(G^{(d)}).$$
   *Moreover, for any certified upper bound $\Lambda_1 > \mu_1(G^{(d)})$ verified by Cholesky factorization of the $d \times d$ matrix $\Lambda_1 I_d - G^{(d)} \succ 0$, we have the strictly algebraic ball-certified operator norm bound*
   $$\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \operatorname{Tr}(K) - \operatorname{Tr}(G^{(d)}) + \Lambda_1.$$
3. **(Specialization to $d = N_{\mathrm{elem}}$: The Full Block-Invariant Step-Subspace Bound):** *When $d = N_{\mathrm{elem}}$, $\operatorname{span}\{q_1, \dots, q_{N_{\mathrm{elem}}}\} = \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$, so $\operatorname{Tr}(G^{(N_{\mathrm{elem}})}) = \operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_{\mathrm{step}} K \Pi_{\mathrm{step}}) = \operatorname{Tr}(K_{00})$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(K) - \operatorname{Tr}(K_{00}) = \operatorname{Tr}((I - \Pi_{\mathrm{step}}) K (I - \Pi_{\mathrm{step}})) = \operatorname{Tr}(K_{\perp\perp})$, yielding*
   $$\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \lambda_{\max}(K_{00}) + \operatorname{Tr}(K_{\perp\perp}),$$
   *where $\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\perp\perp})$ is given by the Woodbury-free elementary Poincaré residual integral*
   $$\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\perp\perp, \mathrm{even}}) = \int_0^{\xi_0} w_-(\xi) \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} \frac{1}{v_k}\left(\int_{I_k} \cos^2(\xi x)\,dx - \frac{1}{\ell_k}\left(\int_{I_k} \cos(\xi x)\,dx\right)^2\right) d\xi.$$

### 1.2 Proof of Theorem 1

**Proof of (1):**  
For any $g \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp = \bigoplus_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} L^2_0(I_k)$ and any $w_m = \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} W_{km} \mathbf{1}_{I_k} \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$, we have $\langle w_m, g \rangle_{L^2(0, b)} = \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} W_{km} \int_{I_k} g(x)\,dx = 0$. Similarly, $V(x) g(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{elem}}} v_k \mathbf{1}_{I_k}(x) g(x)$ has $\int_{I_k} v_k g(x)\,dx = 0$, so $V(x) g \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp$. Thus $P_0 g = V(x) g \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}^\perp$, and since $P_0$ is self-adjoint, $P_0$ also maps $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$ into $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$.

**Proof of (2) and (3):**  
Since $\langle q_i, P_0 q_j \rangle_{L^2} = \delta_{ij}$, the vectors $\tilde{q}_i := P_0^{1/2} q_i \in \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{step}}$ ($i = 1, \dots, d$) form an orthonormal set in $L^2(0, b)$, and
$$\langle \tilde{q}_i, K \tilde{q}_j \rangle_{L^2} = \langle P_0^{1/2} q_i, P_0^{-1/2} A_-^{(\Delta_\alpha)} P_0^{-1/2} P_0^{1/2} q_j \rangle_{L^2} = \langle q_i, A_-^{(\Delta_\alpha)} q_j \rangle_{L^2} = G^{(d)}_{ij}.$$
Thus $G^{(d)}$ is the orthogonal compression of the compact positive self-adjoint operator $K \succeq 0$ to the $d$-dimensional subspace $\operatorname{span}\{\tilde{q}_1, \dots, \tilde{q}_d\}$. By the Cauchy–Poincaré Interlacing Theorem (Courant–Fischer min-max theorem), $\lambda_j(K) \ge \mu_j(G^{(d)}) \ge 0$ for every $j \in \{1, \dots, d\}$. Since $\lambda_j(K) \ge 0$ for all $j > d$,
$$\operatorname{Tr}(K) = \lambda_1(K) + \sum_{j=2}^d \lambda_j(K) + \sum_{j > d} \lambda_j(K) \ge \lambda_1(K) + \sum_{j=2}^d \mu_j(G^{(d)}) = \lambda_1(K) + \operatorname{Tr}(G^{(d)}) - \mu_1(G^{(d)}).$$
If $\Lambda_1 I_d - G^{(d)} \succ 0$ has a valid Cholesky factorization $L L^T = \Lambda_1 I_d - G^{(d)}$ with strictly positive diagonal entries $L_{ii} > 0$, then for every unit vector $u \in \mathbb{R}^d$, $u^T G^{(d)} u = \Lambda_1 - \|L^T u\|_2^2 < \Lambda_1$, so $\mu_1(G^{(d)}) < \Lambda_1$. Substituting $\mu_1(G^{(d)}) < \Lambda_1$ gives $\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}} = \lambda_1(K) < \operatorname{Tr}(K) - \operatorname{Tr}(G^{(d)}) + \Lambda_1$. $\square$
