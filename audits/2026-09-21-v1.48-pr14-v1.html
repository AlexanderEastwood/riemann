1. Proof, in the requested order

NOTE — gap count and monotonicity pass. The value −D is forced by m ≥ −D, m ≤ β and inf β = −D. If n ≤ K negative values occur, they produce n gaps. Inserting an extra level never increases the cost, so QCn ≥ QCK. Positive values do not add negative gaps. The argument in lines 59–71 is correct.

NOTE — Borel domination passes. The displayed definitions make μg and m̃ finite positive Borel measures on [−D,0], of mass at most one. Thus eq:v148-wlh is sufficient as stated: μg − c′m̃ is a positive measure, and domination extends from indicator functions to nonnegative Borel functions by simple-function approximation. Explicitly saying “finite positive Borel measures” would improve clarity, but no extra hypothesis is missing here.

NOTE — packing passes conditionally. It needs m̃ absolutely continuous and 0 < ρ̃max < ∞ as an upper bound for its density. Packing a mass mj into a leftmost interval at density ρ̃max costs at least mj²/(2ρ̃max), and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the displayed bound. v1.47 supplies absolute continuity at a fixed window, not bounded density.

2. Uniform-density constant

NOTE — the constant passes exactly. If d m̃ = (φ−/D) dt, equal gaps attain QCK = φ−D/(2K). Multiplying by c′ gives cD/(2K) with c = c′φ−. There is no missing factor of two.

3. MAJOR findings

M1 — “exactly when” is not proved. Lines 53–54 turn a sufficient lower bound into a necessary-and-sufficient condition. Divergence of φ−²/ρ̃max is sufficient for that bound to force increasing K, not necessary for the exact cost to do so. For example, the probability measure ½U[−D,0] + ½U[−1,0] has peak density tending to ½, hence bounded scalar, but QCK ≥ D/(4K) for every fixed K. This is a countermodel to the inference, not an arithmetic counterexample. Replace the biconditional by a sufficient implication.

M2 — the continuous density peak is not the histogram peak. The coarea formula is ρ̃(t) = X−1Σβ(ξ)=t1/|β′(ξ)| at regular levels. If a negative critical point has β(ξ) = t₀ + A(ξ−ξ₀)r + …, r ≥ 2, its contribution grows like |t−t₀|1/r−1. The essential supremum is infinite. Every complete negative trough inside the range has such a minimum. Thus the “peak near shallow zero” interpretation of a 200-bin histogram is not a property of the exact level density. The script computes bin averages, not an upper bound for ρ̃. The unqualified replacement of ZLD by this scalar in the status, README, NS-30, map and both logs drops a substantive hypothesis and obscures this obstruction. Retain QCK as the valid quantity; retain the density bound only as a conditional lemma.

M3 — λ=4 feasibility is contradicted by the saved output. At c′ = 1 the returned pure state has minimum bin ratio 0.636, not at least 1; the archived output states this directly. Its energy 3.030e−17 is not a feasible upper bound on the constrained minimum. The dual value −1.606e−15 is only an approximate lower diagnostic, not evidence of a feasible cheap state. A mixture in the near-null space might work, but none is provided. The analogous c′=4 claim also fails for the returned vector (ratio 1.845). Remove the claim that these outputs show the exact weighted hypothesis satisfied; even feasible 20-bin states do not establish domination on all Borel sets.

4. Source projection: no factor guarantee

For a raw unit LP vector f, set r = |⟨f,ua⟩|, ε = ‖W ua‖ and g = Paf/√(1−r²). With E = q[f] and δ = (2r+r²)ε,

(E−δ)/(1−r²) ≤ q[g] ≤ (E+δ)/(1−r²).

Neither a certified source nor small ε bounds 1/(1−r²) for this LP vector. A factor-two conclusion would follow, for example, from r² ≤ ¼ and δ ≤ E/2 with E certified positive. Those checks are absent. Fourier mass also changes by the pointwise subtraction in eq:v147-projected-density, so projection need not preserve WLH. The λ=3 source certificate concerns a different vector and cutoff; it does not certify this overlap.
