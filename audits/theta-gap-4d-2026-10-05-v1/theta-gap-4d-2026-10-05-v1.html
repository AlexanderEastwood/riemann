What the missing proof must do

The actual original function selects a very particular path through this four-coordinate space:

r ↦ (r, Y(r), Y′(r), Y″(r)).

We need to show that this path stays on the allowed side at every real height. That path is deliberately not drawn here: a finite plot would not supply the missing all-height proof.

The required lower bound, including its correction:

Y′² − Y·Y″ ≥ c(r)·Y²
c(r) = 2(1/4 − r²)/(r² + 1/4)²

For every real r, for the original theta coefficients.

“Above” means above this lower bound, not above a fixed screen direction. If Y is positive, lowering curvature helps the inequality; if Y is negative, the allowed direction reverses.

Below |r| = 1/2 the correction is positive. Above it, the correction is negative. Omitting it changes the question. At Y = 0 the complete expression is regular and becomes a nonnegative square; no division by Y is needed.

What we have — and what it does not give

The boundary repair is complete. The positive decaying primitive makes the pair moments integrable, and the integrations by parts retain their full boundaries. This is why the displayed expression is legitimate.

The sign estimate is absent. Positivity, decay and a good tail do not force this inequality. The existing NS101 control retains those features yet violates the corrected sign in its established parameter range.

The original arithmetic remains different. That control changes the original theta coefficients and does not obey the same nonlinear Jacobi initial-value problem. It therefore does not disprove the original target. A useful next method must exploit that difference to estimate the complete signed quantity.

This is a partial target. First-Laguerre positivity is only one condition in the broader real-zero problem. The NB cofinal gain and the Weil uniform-floor targets are separate open obligations. This page does not reduce the entire project to one final inequality.

Exact identity and how the 4D picture is drawn

In PR76’s fixed normalization, X(r) = xi(1/2 + ir)/2. Let Theta(x) be the sum of exp(−pi·n²x) over all integers n, let h(u) = exp(u/2)·Theta(exp(2u)), and let f(u) = 2 cosh(u/2) − h(u). Its Fourier transform is Y(r).

p = r² + 1/4
X = pY/4
16(X′² − X·X″)
= p²(Y′² − Y·Y″) + 2(r² − 1/4)Y².

The canvas draws the zero set of the right side in independent coordinates (r, y, v, w). For y ≠ 0, each height sheet has w = v²/y + 2(r² − 1/4)y/p². At y = v = 0 every w lies on the boundary; these lines are included separately. The curvature segment through the selected example marks its allowed and violating portions.

Display ranges are 0 ≤ r ≤ 2, |y| ≤ 1.2, |v| ≤ 1.4 and |w| ≤ 2.2. The renderer samples and clips the boundary, scales these four axes, rotates in four dimensions, then projects to three dimensions and onto the screen. Thus a sheet may end at the display box; it is not a mathematical endpoint.

The coordinate scales are only for viewing. No physical metric, symplectic structure or new geometric theorem is asserted. The sliders do not impose the derivative consistency of a genuine function. For example, the actual even function has Y′(0) = 0, and complete moments already give strict first-Laguerre positivity at r = 0. Free slider settings need not respect that.

Y is the meromorphic completed zeta factor with poles at r = ±i/2; it is not the entire function X. This model uses real r throughout.
