Repairing the theta-pair boundary

The complete theta pair can be rewritten so that every term has a legitimate ordinary Fourier transform. Recovering the original function preserves an explicit sign correction. The arithmetic inequality remains open.

Wall check: Same open gap. Closest: NS100/101 and PR #74–75. This resolves a boundary-accounting question. It supplies no all-height signed estimate.

1. A positive primitive that decays

Keep the complete original theta sum. With h(u)=exp(u/2)*Theta(exp(2u)), define

f(u) = 2*cosh(u/2)-h(u)

(1/4-partial_u^2)*f = 4*phi

f(u) = 4*integral_R exp(-abs(u-v)/2)*phi(v) dv.

The last identity uses the positive Green kernel with decay at both ends. It proves f>0. The complete theta series also gives f(u)<exp(-abs(u)/2), with ratio tending to one in both tails. No finite prefix or artificial cusp is introduced.

2. The pair calculation now has finite terms

Define the two moments of f(s+t)*f(s-t), weighted by 1 and s², as A_0(t) and A_2(t). They and the required derivatives decay exponentially. The complete original associated kernel is

C(t) = [(partial_t^2-1)^2*A_2(t)
        -4*(partial_t^2+1)*A_0(t)]/256.

Every term can now be transformed separately. This addresses PR #75’s growing-moment difficulty while preserving the original kernel and both infinite boundaries.

3. The exact correction must remain

Let Y be the Fourier transform of f, and write p=r²+1/4. Recovering the original X=xi(1/2+ir)/2 gives X=p*Y/4. Two independent algebraic calculations give

L1[Y] = Y'^2-Y*Y''

16*L1[X] = p^2*L1[Y] + 2*(r^2-1/4)*Y^2.

Required, at EVERY real r:
L1[Y] >= 2*(1/4-r^2)/(r^2+1/4)^2 * Y^2.

The correction changes sign at abs(r)=1/2. The formula stays valid at zeros, with no division by Y. Positivity of the real primitive and of its pair moments does not establish this inequality. In fact, Y(r)=-Lambda(1/2+ir), where Lambda(s)=pi^(-s/2)*Gamma(s/2)*zeta(s): this is the completed zeta factor with its quadratic prefactor removed.

4. The existing control retains the repair

The NS101 reciprocal mixture has the same smooth, positive primitive, the same exponential envelope and the same unit leading tail. Yet PR #74’s complete-moment argument makes its first Laguerre expression negative in the stated parameter range. The corrected expression above therefore fails for that control too.

This stops an inference from those shared structural properties. The control changes the original lattice coefficients and loses the distinguished Jacobi equation. It does not refute an estimate that actually uses that missing arithmetic input.

There is also a source-domain qualification: Y has poles at r=+/-i/2. Its kernel has exponential tails, so it falls outside the faster-decay, entire-transform class in Csordas’s Definition 1.2. Those theorems cannot be imported merely from positivity and smoothness.
