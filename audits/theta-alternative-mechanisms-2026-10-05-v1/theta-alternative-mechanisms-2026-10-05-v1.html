The useful next question

For the complete pair of original theta factors, retain the same total mass M and second moment C:

M(z) = integral phi(s+z)*phi(s-z) ds
C(z) = integral s^2*phi(s+z)*phi(s-z) ds
k(z) = C(z)/M(z)

Positive definiteness of k would provide a sufficient certificate for the complete first-Laguerre inequality. PR78 ruled out one positive-mixture representation for k. It left this broader property open.

The new question is precise: does M have a complex zero inside the strip |Im z| < pi/4 at which C is nonzero? Such a zero makes k have a pole. The nearest poles force the Fourier transform of k to change sign, excluding positive definiteness.

Uncancelled zero of M inside the strip
  → pole of k = C/M
  → Fourier(k) changes sign
  → this quotient certificate fails

The independent check includes the complete theta tails, all pole multiplicities and the correct Fourier factors. M is positive on both coordinate axes, so a relevant zero must be off those axes. A small zero-free strip around the real axis already exists qualitatively; that fact alone supplies no sign estimate.

This would not refute the original inequality. The exact target averages the Fourier transform of k against a nonnegative weight. That average can remain nonnegative even when the transform itself changes sign. Likewise, finding no poles in one region would not establish positive definiteness.

What the other two methods established

Heat transport has a subtractive source. We derived the exact evolution and checked its scaling against the standard Gaussian deformation. Moving from a known positive endpoint toward the original function subtracts an accumulated term whose size is unestimated. The simplest favorable-sign assumption fails already at zero height. This auxiliary heat parameter is different from the original nonlinear Jacobi evolution.

The unrestricted duplication matrix fails. Keeping every completed theta sector and cross term produces a matrix with two positive directions and one negative direction wherever X is nonzero. Removing its explicit phase correction leaves exactly the original unknown scalar inequality. This rejects that matrix condition; general nonlinear duplication methods remain open.

A bounded discovery proposal

A locator need not begin with a zero supplied by someone else. It does need its evaluator, controls, error treatment and stopping rule fixed before it runs. The reviewers proposed this possible domain:

1/8 <= Re z <= 2
1/8 <= Im z <= 5/8

At most 9 × 9 initial evaluations
At most 4 cells, 12 refinement steps each
90-minute discovery cap, including controls

This rectangle is a tractability choice, not a prediction of a zero. Test both an uncancelled pole and a removable denominator zero first. A detector that flags M=0 without checking C would give false conclusions.

An apparent root would then need a separate rigorous certificate: complete interval enclosures, a positive argument-principle count for M, exclusion of zero in C, uniform tails and two working precisions. The initial proposed certificate budget is two hours for one box.

No witness means inconclusive. Do not enlarge the search automatically. A certificate would close the general quotient-PD sufficient construction; a failed or inconclusive enclosure would not establish its opposite. The locator itself has not been implemented or run in this review.
