The actual construction

Start with a cylinder: one coordinate t repeats after one unit, while x runs along the real line. Add their two momentum coordinates, τ and y. This gives four dimensions. Use

b(x) = x(1 − x)/(x² − x + 1)
v(x) = (a + c)/2 + (a − c) cos(πx)/2
a = 1/log 2,   c = 1/log 3
H = v(x)τ + b(x)y.

On the energy surface H = 1, the flow is complete and the contact volume never vanishes. Away from x = 0 and x = 1, the x coordinate moves strictly in one direction, so it cannot return. At those two levels, the remaining equations leave exactly one periodic circle each. Their local expansion and contraction give the required two prime-period weights.

This is an explicit model with equations and a classification of its recurrent trajectories. It does not rely on a diagram or on treating a named space as a constructed object. Construction and review.

Why connectedness was too weak a target

The momentum τ is conserved. It equals log 2 on one periodic circle and log 3 on the other. A trajectory therefore cannot approach one circle in the past and the other in the future. Paths connecting them on the base cylinder escape to infinity in the extra momentum direction after lifting to the full energy surface.

The space is connected, but the two trapped orbit components do not interact. The same construction works with arbitrary distinct positive assigned periods. Primality is supplied as an input; this geometry does not explain it.

That corrects the earlier warning about mixed orbits. Mere connectedness does not force them. In a specified two-state model, positive transitions in both directions do add a mixed term; a one-way transition need not. An orbit exactly at log 6 also requires a particular rule for adding travel times. Exact mixing calculation.

A stronger result about the ordinary spectrum

On each of the three open regions separated by x = 0 and x = 1, an explicit coordinate change reduces the contact form and flow to

α = du + τ dθ,    R = ∂/∂u.

The flow is simply translation along u. Those regions cover the entire scalar Hilbert space up to sets of volume zero. Fourier transformation then gives a continuous spectrum covering the real line, with no square-integrable eigenvectors.

This rules out the proposed discrete-spectrum interpretation for the natural scalar realization of this model. It does not rule out every weighted, resonant or geometric approach. In particular, an assigned damped transport on differential forms does reproduce the two local signed orbit contributions. Its distributional trace is a different operation from a positive ordinary Hilbert-space trace. The required analytic realization and full arithmetic completion are still missing.
