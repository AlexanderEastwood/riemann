1. What the projection comparison actually says

Use the complete space L²((0,∞), dt/t²), the original atoms an(t) = A(t/n), and VN = span{a1, …, aN}. Write ΠN for its orthogonal projection. All physical tails remain included.

The dilation Dpf(t) = f(t/p) has squared norm factor 1/p. Thus Up = √p Dp is unitary and UpVN ⊂ VpN. Define

P̃p,N = UpΠNUp*,
Ep,N = ΠpN − P̃p,N.

Because the subspaces are nested, Ep,N is the orthogonal projection onto the additional old directions. For qN(c) = ∑N<n≤2N cnan, put

Δp,N(c) = ‖Ep,NUpqN(c)‖².

Let HN be NS98’s full Schur complement and HpN[p] its counterpart at size pN restricted to the indices pm, pn with N < m,n ≤ 2N. The inherited projection algebra gives

p cTHpN[p]c = cTHNc − Δp,N(c),
0 ≤ Δp,N(c) ≤ cTHNc.

To check the equality, split (I − P̃p,N)Upq into its two orthogonal components (I − ΠpN)Upq and Ep,NUpq. This is standard nested-projection bookkeeping, not a new arithmetic estimate.

The upper bound on Δ still uses the source cost. It supplies no independent constant, vanishing relative error or uniform weighted transfer. The positive matrix ordering does not assign signs to individual off-diagonal differences. NS98’s complete cost and stop record.

2. The prime identity leaves two channels

Keep dN(n) = −μ(n) log(2N/n), without rescaling. Let qN(0) contain only terms with p ∤ n. Möbius multiplicativity gives the exact coefficient identity

qpN = qpN(0) − DpqN(0).

This uses μ(pm) = −μ(m) when p ∤ m and μ(pm) = 0 when p ∣ m. The latter statement is essential; omitting it would incorrectly treat the full coefficient vector as prime-scaled.

Project both channels off VpN. Call the resulting vectors u and v, respectively, so the actual new direction is u − v. Put A = ‖u‖², C = ⟨u,v⟩, and KN(0) = ‖(I − ΠN)qN(0)‖². Then

KpN = A + [KN(0) − Δp,N(dN(0))]/p − 2C.

All three obstacles are visible. A is an unknown cost at the new size pN. KN(0) uses a coefficient mask and is not automatically bounded by KN. The sign and useful magnitude of C remain unestimated. Cauchy–Schwarz gives only

KpN ≤ (√A + √(KN(0)/p))².

This is not a closed recurrence. Even an independent bound on Δ alone would leave the other terms. No weighted cancellation theorem has been transferred to this identity.

3. Why deleting coefficients is not a valid shortcut

An exact abstract control checks the tempting assertion that keeping only the p-free coefficients can only decrease cost. For 0 < ε < 1, take

H = [[1, 1−ε], [1−ε, 1]],
d = (1, −1),   d(0) = (1, 0).

H is positive definite: its determinant is ε(2−ε). Yet dTHd = 2ε while (d(0))THd(0) = 1. Their ratio is unbounded as ε tends to zero. Thus positivity of the Gram does not justify this masking step. This is a control of that generic inference, not a counterexample for the original arithmetic HN or its Möbius vector.

4. Control screen and verification scope

The structural comparison was screened first through the repository’s controls/davenport_heilbronn.py interface. The explicit finite control Gram was

GF(m,n) = (mn)−1/2 ∑k=032 wk cos(tk log(m/n)),
tk = k/2,   wk = |F(itk)|² / ∑ℓ|F(itℓ)|².

This is a positive Gram with the required dilation geometry for both completed xi and the Davenport–Heilbronn control. All tested comparisons passed at 50 and 80 decimal digits for (N,p) = (2,2), (2,3), (4,2); the saved eigenvalues and nonzero-direction ratios agree to 30 displayed digits. These are floating-point diagnostics. They are surrogate Grams, not NS98’s arithmetic Gram, and no quadrature approximation to that Gram is asserted. A zero filtered direction is recorded with a blank ratio.

The full arithmetic target is separately marked not applicable to this Davenport–Heilbronn substitution and to NS100/101: those alterations do not preserve the original fractional-part atoms together with reciprocal-zeta Möbius coefficients. Their structural passes do not establish or refute the original cost estimate.

The existing NS74 same-Gram control preserves every Schur complement and hence this prime-scaling loss matrix, while changing the target loads and allowing a positive approximation floor.
