What positivity gives us—and what refitting removes

The positive divisor-square proposal does not supply the missing lower gain. Used as the entire coefficient rule, it leaves a fixed error floor. Once the old coefficients are free to change, its positive mixtures generate exactly a signed linear space. This identifies where the proposed positivity argument stops.

The result concerns one precisely defined construction. The actual optimized approximation, the arithmetic lower-gain target, G2 and RH remain open.

A genuine square uses least common multiples

If a divisor sum is squared pointwise, its coefficients group at lcm(i,j). This differs from the ordinary-product convolution square tested earlier. For a positive semidefinite matrix Γ, define qℓ by summing Γij over lcm(i,j)=ℓ. Then every divisor sum ∑ℓ|mqℓ is nonnegative, although individual coefficients can be signed.

This is classical Selberg-sieve algebra. The new project test asks what happens when it is used in our fixed approximation space.

After refitting, both signs return

For indices i,j already in the old fit, the two rank-one positive matrices made from ei+ej and ei−ej give the functions

ai + aj ± 2alcm(i,j).

The old-space projection removes the first two terms. Positive mixtures can therefore realize either sign of every available new direction. With unrestricted old refitting, their projected cone is exactly the signed span of those directions.

Consequently the matrix of pairings with an old-orthogonal residual is either zero or indefinite. Positivity of Γ alone forces no sign for the numerator. A fixed trace budget, a normalized square, or a single rank-one square is an additional restriction and is not covered by the equality of cones.

Where the project should go next

Return to the joint signed arithmetic estimate for the actual optimized residual: a lower bound on its available improvement relative to its current error. Keep the complete projected energy and the old-coefficient cancellation in the same estimate. The tail is already controlled at the required scale.

A square-based alternative would need a quantified restriction that survives refitting and still gives the required numerator and cost bounds. Positivity by itself, or another finite efficiency table, does not meet that requirement.
