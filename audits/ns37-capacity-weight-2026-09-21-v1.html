Checks supporting the verdict

NOTE N1 · Exact identities · Lines 44–73. Direct Gaussian integration gives

∫0∞ H(u)uz−1du = z(z−1)π−z/2Γ(z/2)/√3.

Absolute summation for Re z > 1 gives the stated Mellin transform of h. Its super-exponential decay at both ends gives entire continuation. At z = 1 the residue of ζ and π−1/2Γ(1/2) give ∫ ex/2h(x)dx = 1/√3. Evenness then gives Ih = 1/√3. Positivity follows termwise for x ≥ 0 and by evenness for x < 0. The elementary estimate n4e−π(n²−1) ≤ e−2(n²−1) gives the displayed upper constant; the n = 1 term gives the lower constant. No RH assumption enters these calculations.

NOTE N2 · Proved implication from the archived radical input · Lines 121–144. The pole action on the even weight is 2Ihcosh(x/2); its sinh moment vanishes. Zero Weil pairing with every compact smooth test therefore gives the distributional archimedean-minus-prime action −2Ihcosh(x/2). The smooth, locally convergent prime and archimedean expressions identify this distribution with the pointwise action used in Picone's identity. This uses radicality as an inherited input, and does not assume a globally positive operator realization.

NOTE N3 · Exact identities and convergence · Lines 78–96 and 132–144. For compact smooth f, strict positivity of h makes g = f/h compact and smooth. Near the continuous diagonal the squared difference supplies O(|x−y|²), while ρ(|x−y|) is O(1/|x−y|). When an endpoint leaves the compact support, the weight's decay makes the continuous and prime-edge totals finite. The prime sum also converges after local differentiation: polynomial factors in n and log n are dominated by the Gaussian in n. Thus the nonnegative terms are individually summable. With Ih2 = 1/3, the potential and positive pole combine to −(2/3) Varν(g). The remaining sinh square has the negative sign shown. For odd g the mean is zero, but that negative square remains.

NOTE N4 · Exact identity and fixed-window extension · Lines 98–155. Every continuous crossing edge contributes once after the factor 1/2 and both orientations; each prime edge is counted once. This gives exactly Ta/h times |f|². For log n ≥ 2a no two endpoints lie in the open window, matching the strict finite-prime cutoff.

The closure argument is valid on each fixed window. More explicitly, on the physical smooth core,

Jh[f/h] = qa[f] + Va[f],
Va[f] = 2Ih∫Iₐ(c/h)|f|² − Π[f].

Here Va is bounded on L²(Ia) because c/h is bounded and c,s belong to L²(Ia). Consequently suitable shifted form norms are equivalent. For a core-approximating sequence, the squared edge differences are Cauchy in the associated positive edge-measure space; their almost-everywhere limit is the edge difference of f/h. This identifies the closed energy with the displayed integral. The exterior part is nonnegative and at most the total energy, and (ra)− ≤ 2Ihc/h. Both potential parts are therefore integrable on the physical form domain. These boundedness constants may depend on a; no uniform bound on 1/h is used or implied.

NOTE N5 · Proved implication · Lines 205–246. For sufficiently large a, the reflected supports are disjoint, so each parity pair is orthonormal. The linear source functional on the two-dimensional even span has a nontrivial kernel, giving an exactly orthogonal unit vector. No approximation to ua or projection-energy estimate is required. All odd vectors are orthogonal to the even source. The estimates hold for every unit linear combination, including complex coefficients. In particular, Cauchy–Schwarz gives |f̂|² ≤ 2(|φ̂1|² + |φ̂2|²); this covers the archimedean cross terms in the stated A.

NOTE N6 · Proved implication and constants · Lines 181–258. One may take a larger threshold a0 ≥ 3ℓ + b. For a support point x in the positive band, x−ℓ is inside the window and outside both support bands; use the reflected inward edge on the negative band. These selected edges are disjoint and cover the full L² mass. Their prime-2 coefficient and weight ratio multiply to

(log 2)/√2 · (2π−3)/(4π) · 2−9/2

The support minimum x ≥ a−2ℓ−b gives γ = (3π/4)e−4ℓ−2b.
