The identity isolates the arithmetic we still need

The optimized-residual identity gives us a controlled route from finite arithmetic sums to a guaranteed reduction in the complete error. It does not yet prove that the error tends to zero.

The earlier “complete tail bound” controlled the omitted part of one correlation. Combining many correlations needs an additional check: small individual errors can be amplified by the matrix used to calculate improvement. This update controls the combined tail in exactly that matrix’s geometry.

What is now proved

Write A for the improvement indicated by the finite observations and B for a certified allowance for their combined omitted tail. Then the full available improvement is at least:

[max(0, √A − √B)]²

A specified, conservatively damped correction actually achieves this guarantee. All old coefficients are refitted in the correct projection, and the complete arithmetic Gram matrix remains in the calculation.

There is also an asymptotic consequence. Combining our existing coefficient bound with the critical-zero lower bound shows that a physical observation cutoff of N⁶ makes the omitted tail negligible on the proposed 1/log N improvement scale. More generally, N5+ε suffices for every fixed ε > 0.

This is a proved localization result. It supplies no effective starting size, practical solver or decay theorem. The constants are conservative; the asymptotic statement is not used to certify the small examples below. No claim that N² suffices follows.

Where I would go next

On dyadic sizes N = 2j, a lower bound of order a/j for the fraction of total error removed, with a > 0, would suffice if it held eventually. Our earlier lower bound excludes a > 1; smaller positive constants remain possible. No such lower bound is proved.
