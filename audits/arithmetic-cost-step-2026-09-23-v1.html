A direct cost bound and an explicit improving step

The selected direction now has a complete upper cost bound calculated from finite Möbius sums, without the new block’s matrix inverse. A separately integrated update at size 256 reduces the full squared error by more than 1.57% while changing only one old coefficient and the new block. The uniform arithmetic estimate needed for RH remains open.

What the bound does

The earlier work supplies a lower bound for the signed gain numerator. To turn it into an improving step, we also need an upper bound on the direction’s full cost. Previously the sharp cost came from the complete projected matrix.

I applied the complete averaging identity already proved in NS61 to the known Möbius/logarithmic direction. Each signed coefficient tail is summed first. A triangle inequality is then applied to the resulting functions, whose norms already include the whole infinite domain.

The result is an explicit scalar bound, UN. It upper-bounds both the true projected cost and the cost of a feasible direction that adjusts only the old coefficient at N. The latter direction vanishes exactly before N. No new-block inverse or refit of the other old coefficients is needed to execute the step. The starting optimized old coefficients remain inherited inputs.

What remains unproved

The cost calculation reduces to weighted squares of finite Möbius tail sums. Seven certified scalar budgets, at sizes 16, 64, 256, 1,024, 4,096, 16,384 and 65,536, lie between 0.00098 and 0.00220. This finite screen does not establish an eventual bound.

Partial summation makes the missing assumption explicit: square-root cancellation in the block’s summatory Möbius function would give a bounded budget. It is not proved or assumed here. The elementary estimate gives only a budget growing proportionally to N. A bounded scalar budget, together with a persistent signed numerator margin, would imply a sufficient a/j-scale gain on N = 2j; both inputs remain open.

The original projected cost may be substantially smaller than this upper budget. The next estimate should preserve useful old-space cancellation if the scalar budget becomes too expensive. Its failure would not close PR42’s full joint-observation route. RH, G2, and the uniform arithmetic lower-gain problem remain open.
