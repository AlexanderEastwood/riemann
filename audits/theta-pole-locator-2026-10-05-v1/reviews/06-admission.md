## 1. What the experiment actually decided

The fixed domain was `[1/8,2]+i*[1/8,5/8]`, strictly inside the justified
theta strip and off both axes. The archived original run used the promised
81 initial vertices, four selected cells, and same-cell refinement/replay.
The 171 evaluations took about 48.46 seconds. No extra region was searched.
The independently implemented replay subsequently evaluated only the four
already recorded final endpoints; it did not move them or select new cells.

Their registered 256-bit residual ratios were approximately

```text
0.03914164, 0.54809427, 0.49442807, 0.64851256,
```

against the fixed threshold `2^-40`, approximately `9.095e-13`. All four
paths stopped because the next safeguarded Newton proposal would leave its
selected cell. Agreement between the two precision paths therefore means
agreement between stalled paths, not agreement between approximate roots.
The minimum reported residual is a value at one endpoint, not a lower
bound anywhere else in its cell or the registered domain.

The four-corner sampled windings all rounded to zero. Those samples do not
control the phase between corners, including after the nonvanishing
analytic normalization. Refining only four cells cannot provide coverage
of all possible zeros. Neither the finite null nor contact with a cell
boundary supports a statement that a zero lies just outside the region.

Review 04 independently replaced the lattice recurrence by direct theta
exponentials and composite Gauss-Legendre by adaptive tanh-sinh, with a
larger lattice cutoff and integration endpoint. The largest observed
relative discrepancies were about `8.38e-29` for normalized M, `1.41e-26`
for normalized C, and `1.05e-26` for the normalized M derivative. This is
useful stability evidence for the four tested values and is consistent
with their failure to approach the registered root threshold. It is not
a rigorous error bound, a regional result, or evidence for 256 bits of
numerical accuracy. The quadrature routines' much smaller heuristic error
numbers must not replace this qualification.

## 2. The rejection implication was never triggered

The intended conditional edge retains the full original functions:

```text
M(z)=integral_R phi(s+z)*phi(s-z) ds,
C(z)=integral_R s^2*phi(s+z)*phi(s-z) ds,
k=C/M.

Original M(z0)=0 and C(z0)!=0, abs(Im z0)<pi/4
  => an uncancelled pole of k
  => Fourier(k) changes sign by PR79's complete-strip argument
  => k is not positive definite.
```

PR79's hypotheses include the original complex-strip continuation, axis
nonvanishing, sector-uniform end asymptotics and complete contour/residue
accounting. A finite prefix, an approximate zero, or a sampled winding
cannot replace them. The locator retained the original arithmetic and
supplied analytic truncation accounting, but did not produce an original
denominator-zero witness.

Numerator values on these non-root paths decide nothing about cancellation.
Even a small denominator would require a separate numerator assessment:
failure to exclude zero from C is unresolved, not proof of cancellation.
The matched synthetic controls correctly exercised this distinction. The
same denominator `z^4+1` was located in both cases; numerator 1 passed the
candidate gate, while numerator `(z^4+1)*exp(-z^2)` did not receive an
uncancelled-pole label. That validates the tested classification behavior,
not universal root-finding reliability or original-theta evidence.

An actual certified pole would reject only the sufficient quotient-PD
construction. The exact identity

```text
L1[X](r)=(1/pi)*integral_R X(r-xi/2)^2*Fourier(k)(xi) dxi
```

can have nonnegative output with a signed kernel. Hence even successful
pole certification would not refute the original first-Laguerre target;
the current null gives still less. First-Laguerre positivity alone is not
an RH proof.

## 3. Complete tails are distinct from a complete numerical enclosure

The lattice and real-integration tail formulas retain both shifted
factors, the quadratic weight for C, derivative terms for M', and the
normalization derivative. Reviews 03/05 found no omission relevant to this
diagnostic. They are analytic truncation majorants whose displayed values
are evaluated with ordinary floating arithmetic. Finite quadrature error
and arithmetic roundoff have not been enclosed.

This distinction prevents small tail numbers from being read as complete
function enclosures. The independent replay increases confidence in four
sampled values but supplies neither outward rounding nor boundary control.
No zero-free boundary, positive rigorous argument-principle count, or
numerator exclusion on a box was established. No interval certificate was
started, and the conditional certificate budget should remain unused.
