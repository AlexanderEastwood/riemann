MINOR m1 — Two real bumps do not cover an arbitrary complex source

Location. prop:v130-local-no-go, especially its final fixed-family argument.

Claimed. For every normalized complex L² source uₐ, a normalized real even test gₐ can be chosen in the displayed two-dimensional family with gₐ ⟂ uₐ.

Supported. Complex orthogonality imposes two real constraints. Let g₁,g₂ be orthonormal real images of the two bumps and u=(g₁+i g₂)/√2. No nonzero real combination αg₁+βg₂ is orthogonal to u. Thus the written two-dimensional argument fails for the stated quantifier. The actual repaired source is real, so this does not break the intended application; the theorem is repairable and is not used to prove W₄ ≥ 0 or the concentration criterion.

Minimal fix. Restrict uₐ to real sources, or use three linearly independent real bumps. For the final finite-codimension statement, count complex constraints as two real constraints.

6. What the closed routes actually have in common

The proposed universal diagnosis is too broad. Many failures discard sign, phase, joint overlap or spectral location, but they do not all fail for the same reason. A theorem saying that every argument “forgetting arithmetic location” cannot prove a cofinal sign is false without defining the retained information and the class of forms: nonnegative symbols, or sufficiently small negative sets with controlled concentration, can be settled by coarse information.

Route familyActual obstruction established in this manuscript
Unsigned domination / norm contraction / scalar far majorantTaking absolute values destroys cancellation; the resulting prime norm and far-cut cost can be too large. The λ=5 finite shifted counterexample is stronger than merely a bad remote cutoff.
Negative-level trace / Hilbert–Schmidt / Schatten hierarchyDrops the favorable part of β. The signed Dirichlet model is nonnegative while the negative-only penalty stays positive under reciprocal dilation.
Sampling / generic Logvinenko–SeredaRequires quantitative thickness or coverage and constants that pay the signed weight budget. Formal applicability of an uncertainty theorem is insufficient.
Band commutation / Cotlar / atomizationNesting and source removal need not make commutators small. Even exact commutation gives no weighted coverage. Exact atomization retains the same joint Gram obligation unless an additional estimate is proved.

7. Odd parity and the meaning of an even result

The existing full-space identity can be rearranged without a new estimate:

c(x)=cosh(x/2), s(x)=sinh(x/2).

8. Current literature: applicability, not name matching

Screened as of 21 September 2026. Primary sources below supply useful conditional tools, but none inspected supplies the actual cofinal signed inequality for superlevel sets of this prime discrepancy symbol. Annihilating pairs and restriction estimates — directly relevant, conditional

Jaming, Iosevich and Mayeli, “Uncertainty Principle, annihilating pairs and Fourier restriction” (2025), Theorem 2.2, gives a quantitative annihilating-pair constant when a restriction constant ρ satisfies A=ρ|S|1/p−1/2|Σ|1/2−1/q<1. Apply with physical S=(−a,a) and a finite bad set Σ. For a supported test the good-set energy is at least (1−A)² times its norm squared. Such lower bounds can feed the weighted minorant if their weighted sum reaches Dₐ−ηₐ. Missing: uniform restriction constants and suitable geometry for the actual prime-defined bad sets. Its finite-group/additive-energy examples cannot be transferred automatically to real logarithmic-prime superlevels.

Thickness and local density — useful only with quantitative arithmetic input

Kovrijkine, “Some results related to the Logvinenko–Sereda theorem”, controls bandlimited mass on thick sets. Here ℱf is bandlimited because f is physically supported. One must prove thickness of the favorable frequency sets at an explicit scale and track the dependence on a, then meet the signed weighted budget. Neither “the set is nonempty” nor qualitative relative density suffices. Source compression only reduces the class of tests; it does not create the missing arithmetic thickness estimate.

Husain and Littmann, “Concentration inequalities for Paley–Wiener spaces”, discusses local-density bounds; its §2 recalls an L² estimate of the form concentration ≤ (bandwidth + δ⁻¹)·supx|E∩[x,x+δ]|, with normalization-dependent constants. This could bound negative-level concentration if arithmetic local densities were controlled. Its main new L¹ statements must not simply be substituted for an L² source-compressed operator bound. No density bound for the manuscript’s sets follows from that paper.

Recent optimal concentration preprint — scalar upper bound, not signed closure

Abreu and Speckbacher, “Optimal concentration in the Paley–Wiener space” (21 July 2026), Theorem 1.1, claims that an interval maximizes L² concentration among sets of fixed measure for interval bandwidth. If used, it bounds uncompressed concentration by a prolate interval value; source compression is no larger. This audit checked the theorem’s stated scope, not the full proof of this recent preprint. Even accepting it, one still needs measure bounds for every arithmetic level and a cofinal signed budget. A sharper negative-only scalar bound does not escape the manuscript’s model obstruction by itself.

Arithmetic and fractal uncertainty — related subjects, unmatched hypotheses

Granville and Soundararajan, “An uncertainty principle for arithmetic sequences”, concerns distribution in short intervals and arithmetic progressions. Its arithmetic uncertainty is not the L² time–frequency concentration inequality for sets cut out by this continuous prime cosine sum. It cannot be cited as the missing concentration estimate without a further bridge.

Dyatlov and Jin, “Resonances for open quantum maps and a fractal uncertainty principle”, obtains uncertainty for structured fractal sets. No corresponding porosity, regularity or multiscale fractal structure has been established for these prime-sum level sets. Its conclusion is therefore not currently available here.

Literature verdict: the annihilating-pair formulation is mathematically natural and existing tools give conditional bounds. The missing arithmetic geometry/coverage estimate remains exactly the load-bearing step. I found no inspected primary source that supplies it with the window dependence needed for ηₐ→0.
